# Native procedural quality audit — 18 October 2026

Scope: current Cyrus Arrange placement/composition and the native24 snapshot, compared with official SideFX documentation and the pinned native Clipper implementation. This began as read-only research. Root subsequently authorized the two narrow correctness repairs below. No Max process or computer-use tool was launched by this work. Source statements below are direct code observations; proposed next steps are engineering judgments, not claims that Houdini internally uses the same algorithms.

## Two reproduced correctness issues and their narrow repair

### 1. Clustering could cross a regional policy boundary

In the old [candidate displacement](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrangement.cpp:555), ordinary scatter chose a cell, then sometimes replaced XY with a location around an existing prop. Its region ID stayed unchanged. Old membership validation covered protected bases and rows only. The [fit query](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/placement_queries.cpp:252) correctly checks a global horizontal support union, so a same-height neighboring support could pass geometry while the candidate retained the first region's rules.

The frozen native24 repro has A 30 × 30 mm centered at (0,0), B 80 × 80 mm centered at (60,0), tops Z0 and 5 mm cells. One locked 20 × 20 × 10 mm Accessory sits at A's center; density 1, empty 0, cluster 1, edge 0, spacing 0, fixed scale/yaw. Seed 1 accepted an A-tagged prop at (31.876,20.1415,0), physically on excluded B. Locking those outputs made the next Generate fail its membership guard. This is an inconsistency, not a preference about visual composition.

The repair adds the existing charged membership scan after ordinary clustering displacement. Direct cell+jitter construction stays within its chosen sampled square and avoids redundant scans. The guard rejects an out-of-region new anchor as Boundary, preserves protected failure semantics, and leaves full footprint/OBB checks to the query. This certifies sampled anchor ownership; it is not an exact per-region polygon coverage certificate.

Why the procedural precedent matters: SideFX's Scatter documentation explicitly distinguishes per-primitive placement and retention during relaxation; topology-independent authored seed attributes are also distinct from primitive numbers. Persistent identity, geometric fit and ownership should stay separate contracts in this plugin. [SideFX Scatter](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/scatter.html).

Evidence: [before receipt](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/build/quality-admission-before/receipt.json), [before output](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/build/quality-admission-before/before.log), [same probe after repair](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/build/quality-admission-after/after.log), and the `clustered_region_membership` integrated suite. The suite checks excluded and category-masked B across 32 seeds, analytical whole accessory bounds on A, subsequent locking/Generate, and bounded work. No node names or SDK state are part of this numerical proof.

### 2. Round clearance expansion was admitted after it allocated

The old [containment code](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/placement_queries.cpp:85) bounded input outline complexity, called `InflatePaths`, then admitted the larger output. Large clearance around a hole can produce many arc vertices before the output check throws. A four-rectangle ring with outer bounds ±3,000,000 mm and a ±500,000 mm central hole, clearance 1,000,000 mm and unchanged .00025 mm arc tolerance reaches the old post-expansion complexity exception. The expansion is roughly 140,000 vertices despite the tiny input.

The repair [preflights raw joins](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/placement_queries.cpp:45), following the pinned [ClipperOffset source](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/third_party/clipper2/src/clipper.offset.cpp:317). It strips duplicate endpoints as Clipper does, determines group orientation, distinguishes the three-point inward convex branch from actual rounded reflex/hole joins, and bounds `ceil(steps_per_radian * abs(turn))` plus endpoints and a rounding reserve. It charges scans/output and rejects excessive estimated join vertices or remaining quadratic polygon work before offset construction. It preserves the exact requested integer radius and arc tolerance. Rectangular supports with the same huge clearance remain admitted; no all-corners-circle bound rejects them.

The official arc-tolerance documentation warns that very small segments can hurt performance and rounding quality. That supports explicit admission and measurement; it does not justify silently reducing an artist's clearance or changing precision. [Clipper ArcTolerance](https://www.angusj.com/clipper2/Docs/Units/Clipper.Offset/Classes/ClipperOffset/Properties/ArcTolerance.htm).

Limit: this bounds admitted raw join tessellation, not all temporary storage in Clipper's finishing union. Result admission remains necessary, and conservative rejection can occur even if cleanup would simplify the outline. Regression cases distinguish the giant ring from a convex rectangle, reverse parent-patch orientation, test remaining work, and independently check valid/invalid Euclidean clearance around an ordinary L-shaped notch.

## Remaining concrete performance and usability risks

1. **Geometry capture/query rebuild on every Generate.** [prepare_generation](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrange_bridge.cpp:205) recaptures furniture and bound assets before constructing a new query. That is correct for freshness but expensive on imported assemblies. Profile capture, inspection/registry, query build, solve and publication separately. A future immutable prepared-query cache needs explicit time/units/transforms/geometry/obstacle/profile invalidation, not only a document revision. Embree's documented commit-before-query sequencing is a useful ownership pattern, not evidence that this plugin needs Embree. [Embree rtcCommitScene](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RenderKit/embree/master/doc/src/api/rtcCommitScene.md).

2. **Repeated inset calculation per candidate.** [contained](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/placement_queries.cpp:85) copies/offsets the support outline for every member probe. The new preflight prevents the identified unadmitted arc expansion, but does not remove repeated valid work. If timings confirm it dominates, a bounded immutable cache keyed by quantized plane/inset and fixed source query is the smallest targeted improvement. Parent is evaluating performance separately; this audit does not introduce that cache.

3. **Accepted-prop scan and row scratch are linear.** [validate](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/placement_queries.cpp:258) scans accepted OBBs. [row_scratch](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrangement.cpp:526) copies them when staging rows; height balance also scans regional props for each bounded trial. Near-cap scenes can exhaust quadratic work well before 10,000 placements. A bounded broad-phase prop index is a candidate only after real scaling measurements; current caps are not speed claims.

4. **Source axes versus authored spine face.** [capture_mesh](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrange_bridge.cpp:61) bakes the object world basis; [capture_asset](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrange_bridge.cpp:98) subtracts translation/recenters physical bounds without undoing source rotation. A source rotation or mirror can therefore change the actual physical spine direction while an unchanged cardinal profile still names its old captured axis. This is an authoring/rebind risk, not proof of a fit failure. Add a focused host test and explain that spine side refers to the captured asset frame; decide explicitly whether a source edit requires profile re-authoring.

5. **A pose lock is not output-node identity preservation.** [publication](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrange_bridge.cpp:247) recreates outputs then deletes previous nodes, including protected poses. Native IDs and saved poses persist; arbitrary external node references, per-node overrides or constraints may not. Keep the artist contract explicit and add a host test before promising those external relationships survive regeneration. This audit does not change SDK ownership/publication.

6. **Category weights are per eligible asset, not a normalized category quota.** [selection](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrangement.cpp:465) multiplies category and asset weights. Three Book assets plus one Plant at equal weights imply 75% aggregate Book selection, absent other restrictions. The category dialog's [Weight label](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/scripts/ArrangeWorkflow.ms:433) can be misunderstood as 50/50 categories. Show effective aggregate weights or add a precise tooltip; changing the solver distribution would require an intentional new contract and golden qualification.

7. **Height balance is a defined local metric.** [balance](F:/Cursor/_Cyrus_Apps/CyrusScatter/CyrusArrange/src/arrangement.cpp:590) uses 16 bins of maximum top height within one region, keeps authored scales and tests 8 translations plus the original. This is bounded and independently tested, but can have bin-boundary discontinuities and is not whole-furniture symmetry, camera composition, volume/mass balance or physical book stability. Use unchanged-output renders and artist review to judge whether the control is practically useful before adding a larger optimizer.

8. **Conservative fit has known false rejections.** Full asset OBBs can block concave accessories/trays that their detailed meshes would fit around. OBB spacing is a separating-axis margin rather than exact Euclidean corner distance. Front-access sweep is horizontal mesh clearance, not visibility of a TV screen or a complete insertion plan among existing generated props. These are documented numerical limits; artist-authored reserved volumes/category caps can address practical cases without pretending the system understands every interior function.

Performance recommendations above are proposals, not measured speedups. A native C++ implementation does not itself establish fast execution; measurements must isolate the bottleneck. [C++ Core Guidelines, Per.6](https://isocpp.github.io/CppCoreGuidelines/CppCoreGuidelines#per6-dont-make-claims-about-performance-without-measurements).
