01 Findings and recommendation

Status: research synthesis and proposed scope. Source families: R01-R04 engineering studies, R08/R11 preparation studies, R09/R10 practical workflows and R12-R14 solver-reuse research. The final supplied study is included; all fourteen original files are preserved. See the source register and corrections.

Feasibility

The original idea is technically plausible: keep grass planted beside a stone and bend the nearby vegetation into a more natural arrangement. A useful first product is a static geometric result obtained through a bounded fitting operation. The artist can press Update while an internal numerical method performs several steps. Continuous timeline simulation is not a prerequisite for that interaction.

Houdini documents both a static curve collision operation and a Vellum mode that rolls inputs forward internally for each evaluated frame. These establish relevant building blocks, not a proven Cyrus implementation or a guarantee for arbitrary plant meshes. Guide Collide With VDB, Vellum Solver.

The hardest uncertainties are source preparation, reliable initial-intersection recovery, and delivery of differently bent geometry through Max's viewport/render/Bake paths. A convincing guide-only demonstration would leave all three partially unanswered.

R11 strengthens the representation evidence with two supplied public meshes, synthetic counterexamples and reported arithmetic checks. Independent topology recounts agree with its public-asset counts. This supports conservative preparation and explicit identity mapping; it does not establish automatic blade recognition, successful contact fitting or production asset coverage.

R13 adds a preserved C++ reproduction package and reported Linux headless diagnostics. It demonstrates, within that recorded build, a stock rod crossing an obstacle, ambiguous mesh occupancy, tiny-scale query misses and residual penetration after a rigid body sleeps. We inspected its harness/results and matched 48 cited file hashes to the local pinned dependency; we did not repeat the engine runs. This is stronger evidence for the identified gaps, not a complete fitted-grass or Max result. See the evidence review.

R14 adds a different CPU build profile, seven reported native records and nine analytic fixtures with fifteen stored checks. Its three-point rod still crosses a sphere at both 5 and 50 soft iterations; its free interior rod can be ejected with continuing velocity rather than settle into an acceptable shape. It narrows the public callback boundary and strengthens immutable query-generation ownership. The R14 review preserves these findings without treating them as our reproduced runs or as a completed fitter.

A leaf modeled as one rigid body cannot fold over a curb. A single clump guide can provide a broad push but cannot promise individual blade contact on opposite sides of a stone. A solver choice should follow the intended representation and visible result.

First product hypothesis

Use one prepared grass source with explicit roots, ordered guides, base frames and a verified guide-to-mesh binding. Begin with a small set of fixed solid primitive/convex/compound obstacles, plus explicitly declared terrain surfaces where qualified. Arbitrary mesh-solid containment needs a separate gate. Fit only affected accepted placements after ordinary placement acceptance has completed. Keep untouched placements on the existing shared-source path and attach optional deformation state to affected identities.

A focused deterministic fitter with explicit whole-segment/envelope contact is the leading hypothesis. Compare a direct triangle BVH with Jolt shape queries as its contact backend, and retain a small rod/constraint mechanics comparator. R12 and our pinned-source checks identify sampled-vertex contact and root-mass coupling in the stock CPU rod path; existing rigid-body integration does not remove those gaps. Select the method from contact quality, failure behavior, total update cost and output fidelity, not dependency reuse alone.

R13 favors selective Jolt query reuse, conditional on a qualified geometry adapter. Keep the BVH comparison: its reported tiny-scale and containment failures mean query availability alone is insufficient. Compare a fitter and rods against the same final validator, including the contact adapter each actually needs. The reported one-segment hard-clamped rod is a detection diagnostic; it cannot fairly rank feasible routing quality or speed.

The final decision remains a narrow experiment, not a production solver adoption: test a short prepared guide with enough root freedom and length for a known feasible route around one stone. Implement the missing contact response, then test visible width and two obstacles. Pair every intended rejection with a feasible control that must succeed; an implementation that rejects every plant does not meet the requested feature. Keep the authored viewport/render/Bake feasibility experiment early.

Start with artist-facing controls for obstacle selection, clearance, flexibility, root attachment and Update. Add complexity when the fixtures demonstrate a need. No arbitrary-asset automation, full ecosystem simulation, GPU requirement or universal renderer claim is justified by the present evidence.

What “closest objects” should mean

Use proximity to find a bounded neighborhood, then test the actual geometry. The nearest object origin or nearest root is insufficient: a long blade can touch a farther stone, and two stones can constrain the same blade simultaneously. Candidate search must account for plant reach, obstacle bounds and the permitted deformation envelope.

The solver needs all relevant contacts in that neighborhood. Choosing only the closest stone or averaging opposing normals can produce an apparently smooth shape that still penetrates another obstacle.

Proceed to a later, explicitly authorized end-to-end prototype if the user chooses implementation. First demonstrate one bent blade beside one fixed stone, with matching Max 2027 viewport, selected final renderer and Bake output. In parallel, audit a small representative asset set. 02 Artist workflow

Status: proposed behavior for discussion and later qualification. Existing Manual/Live and publication contracts continue to apply. Names below are working labels, not delivered controls.

First use

  1. Select a Scatter layer or an explicitly supported subset of its accepted placements.
  2. Choose Rooted fit and a prepared source preset. Review root positions, base frames and blade/guide associations in an overlay.
  3. Add stones or other supported geometry to an explicit obstacle set. Preview the actual collision representation and clearance envelope.
  4. Adjust clearance and flexibility; choose a fixed base frame or soft angular attachment. Root position remains planted in rooted mode.
  5. Press Update. Review affected, unchanged and unresolved placements, then keep the completed result or revise the request.

Source preparation should be reusable. Moving a stone should generally require another fit, not another mesh binding. A source topology/reference change can require preparation again.

Rigid Settle needs gravity, hulls, friction and a declared start state. Drape needs surface topology, thickness, stretch and optional self-contact. Their controls should be presented with their respective modes.

After moving a stone

Manual mode marks relevant vegetation Pending and retains the last complete publication until explicit Update. That retained geometry may intersect the newly moved stone; its status must show that it is stale. Update includes the stone's old affected region, new affected region and previously dependent plants, so vegetation released from contact can return toward its rest shape.

Live mode schedules bounded work for relevant dependencies. Interactive edits can be coalesced, but a relevant animated obstacle or source cannot be ignored under a blanket “after drag release only” rule. Navigation, selection and unrelated animation must not refit static vegetation or upload unchanged buffers. These follow the existing F04 evaluation contract.

A proposed Freeze at sampled time option would need its own explicit time/dependency contract. It must not silently redefine existing Live behavior. Bake is a distinct output action with deliberate source and dependency retention rules.

Failure to find a local solution is not proof that no possible three-dimensional shape exists. Reserve a proven infeasible label for a specific contradiction, such as a protected root inside a forbidden volume under the declared rules.

Partial acceptance is a product choice

The safe default for an unaccepted attempt is to retain the previous complete result. A useful later option could publish a complete generation containing successful fits and explicitly flagged exceptions. Such an option needs a documented choice of compatible previous shape or rest shape for every failed placement. It must preserve the accepted population, label stale exceptions and allow selection of their IDs.

This is different from exposing unfinished worker buffers. A partial-acceptance policy must still publish atomically and must never silently delete plants, move roots or lower requested clearance.

Reset, save and Undo

Reset to source removes the fit while preserving authored placements. Update starts from the declared source/rest state; use of a prior result as an optimization must preserve the declared repeatability policy. Continue simulation from current state would be a separate future operation.

Describe whether the selected mode validates only the completed static shape or also promises a collision-free path while the plant moves. Whole-segment clearance at one pose does not establish continuous clearance during deformation. The first static mode should make only the supported completion promise.

Save/load must distinguish authored preparation, current fit, stale fit and unavailable dependencies. Undo/Redo must restore the corresponding settings and completed output together, within a measured memory policy. File loading, viewport reading and rendering should consume a completed publication rather than silently solve a new arrangement.

03 Source preparation

Status: proposed asset contract and audit method, with scoped offline representation evidence. R08 supplies the earlier preparation study; R11 adds a concrete framed-guide mapping, two public assets and supplied arithmetic/topology results. No production asset library, automatic preparation system or Max delivery was qualified. See the R11 evidence review.

Choose the representation deliberately

TierSuitable usePreparation and limitation
A: whole-clump guide/cageBroad deflection for middle or distant viewsFew controls; cannot guarantee independent blade paths around a stone
B: blade/card guides or ribbonsClose grass, narrow gaps and multiple obstaclesExplicit membership, roots and frames; strongest initial product candidate for prepared assets
C: authored advanced driverBranches, wide leaves, complex scans and existing rigsSpecialized cage, skeleton or surface binding; separate qualification

The first source should be intentionally prepared. Automatic discovery can be investigated after an audit identifies which geometry families actually dominate the intended library.

Preparation record

Store one immutable record per compatible source revision, with the following information:

  • Source identity, geometry/topology signature, reference pose, units and local coordinate system.
  • Component/blade membership, declared root positions or patches, root tangents and width/frame directions.
  • Ordered rest guides or driver surface, rest lengths, sample spacing and a declared frame convention.
  • Mesh-to-driver correspondence, weights or local coordinates, unbound vertices and cross-piece restrictions.
  • Collision envelope policy, supported scale/transform range and any protected attachment region.
  • UV/map channels, material IDs, smoothing and explicit-normal policy, plus preparation version and diagnostics.
  • Separate render-corner, logical-position and semantic-part identities; verified duplicate correspondence and sidedness classification.
  • Fixed rest binding coordinates, complete residual offsets, rest directors and any unwrapped twist; shared-base and parent/child attachment policies.

This is a proposed data contract, not a new serialized schema. Existing recipe and scene formats require a separate design decision before extension.

Automatic preparation limits

Disconnected mesh components can be useful candidates for separate blades. A connected component is not necessarily a botanical blade: joined roots, welded cards, branching stems and scans can violate that assumption. Conversely, UV seams or split shading normals can divide vertex indices without dividing the physical surface.

An optional virtual weld must use explicit tolerances, preserve analysis provenance and show ambiguous merges. A root-cut analysis graph may help separate joined blades while leaving the original mesh intact. Neither method should destructively detach or rewrite the artist's source.

Each OBJ triangle also has an opposite-winding mate using the same position IDs. Such explicit two-sided construction can inflate raw face-incidence counts. A reversible analysis-only representative can simplify that graph while preserving every original render face and channel. Do not classify the source as corrupt, or delete its back faces, merely from a raw nonmanifold-edge count. Deliberately independent coincident blades must remain separate logical positions.

A shared vertex has one output position. Two independent blade drivers cannot assign it incompatible motion. Choose an explicit common base, pinned transition, reviewed blend or a separately authorized topology change. For attached leaves, evaluate the attachment in the parent's current frame; a child attachment is not a pin to its old world position.

Simple ribbons can support a root-to-tip traversal if width, branching and ordering checks pass. A height threshold is acceptable for a controlled upright fixture, not a general terrain or curved-source root finder. Show suggested roots and frames before treating them as accepted preparation.

Opacity cards contain geometry and a shading silhouette. One textured card showing several grass blades can bend as a card; it does not provide independent geometry for every pictured blade. Alpha-pixel collision and reconstruction are outside the first scope.

Opaque renderer proxies require a verified path to full canonical geometry and attributes. A coarse preview representation is insufficient for a source contract or final contact validation.

Frames and mesh decoding

A centerline supplies position and tangent. It needs a cross-section direction to determine ribbon orientation. Two fixed collinear root samples constrain a tangent but leave axial roll free. A noncollinear base patch can define a geometric frame. For a soft base, the root position can remain fixed while a separate angular constraint permits bending.

Use one explicitly tested decoder first. A framed single-guide mapping or a small ribbon driver is easier to verify than a universal skin/cage abstraction. Interpolation must not blend nearby independent blades merely because their vertices are close.

For vendor comparisons, SideFX warns that ordinary Point Deform is unsuitable for wire models; a piece attribute does not remove that limitation. A curve-aware framed decoder or an actual surface/volume cage needs its own correspondence checks. Point Deform, Wire Deform.

Dual-quaternion skinning can be a useful option, but a fixed four-influence DQ design is not established as the right universal decoder. Matching an imported Max Skin result also requires its deformation mode and per-vertex DQ blend behavior, not only bone weights. ISkinContextData.

Framed-offset reference candidate from R11

R11 proposes a specific mapping for already prepared bindings. For each logical vertex, store a fixed rest-guide coordinate, nonnegative normalized influence weights and the complete offset in the rest material frame. Retain the longitudinal offset as well as width/thickness. At evaluation, move that offset by the accepted guide center and frame, blend the influences, then apply the placement transform once.

The proposed mapping can be written as:

  • d_ik = transpose(R0_k(s_ik)) * (p0_i - C0_k(s_ik))
  • q_i = sum_k w_ik * (C_k(s_ik) + R_k(s_ik) * d_ik)
  • p_i = a_i * p0_i + (1 - a_i) * q_i

Here p0 is the rest position, C/R are guide centers/frames, s is a fixed rest coordinate, w is an influence weight and a is a mesh-patch hold mask. Guide-root orientation is an upstream constraint. A hard patch hold is not a tunable angular spring, and a soft hold blend can shrink geometry.

Use the same sampler at capture and evaluation; do not reproject vertices to newly nearest guide points after bending. R11's example separates shortest-path director interpolation from unwrapped twist so complete turns are retained. It consumes authored frames: it does not build or validate a physical rod frame, guarantee alignment with the interpolated centerline tangent, or solve contact.

Rest-to-rest reconstruction is a preparation gate. Single-guide, equal-coordinate cross-sections preserve width only under the stated proper-frame and zero-hold conditions. Multiple influences can collapse width; R11 includes an intentional counterexample. Representative stress poses must detect these failures. Imported Skin/cage behavior must pass conversion/pose comparisons before this mapping can replace it. This remains a reference candidate, not a selected universal production evaluator.

For a CPU rod comparator, R13 adds a separate solver-index contract. Rod-property calculation can reverse endpoints and optimization can reorder constraints. Keep semantic guide/segment identities mapped through those changes. Engine-generated transported frames do not recover authored blade width or twist. Build fresh rest settings, derive the ordinary properties, then apply any validated material-frame overrides and recompute consistent relative rest rotations. Prefilling a frame alone can skip derived work; inspect the exact implementation and prove rest reconstruction. Rod data and remaps.

Appearance and geometry parity

Verify that decoding preserves vertex/face association, UV seams, mapping channels, material IDs and intentionally edited foliage normals. Recomputing geometric normals can change the appearance of an asset with authored normals. Rotating every source normal by a guide frame is also not universally equivalent to the normal of a blended deformation. Choose and test the policy with the actual materials and lighting.

R11 distinguishes geometric-normal reconstruction from an explicitly artistic transport of authored corner normals. A weighted rotation blend is not the exact inverse-transpose deformation Jacobian. Preserve corner splits, diagnose near-zero blended normals, rebuild tangents under a declared convention and qualify the result through the actual renderer and Bake path. Its supplied tests cover geometric placement-normal arithmetic, not general authored-normal preservation.

Nonuniform scale, mirroring and shear need an explicit rule. A circular local envelope may become elliptical in world space, and normals require the appropriate transform. The first prototype may reject unsupported transforms with a clear diagnosis; it must not silently assume uniform positive scale.

Invalidation and asset audit

Rebuild preparation when source topology, reference pose, root metadata, membership, guide construction or binding semantics change. UV/material edits can remain appearance-only only if those channels were not used to discover roots, membership, stiffness or other geometry behavior.

A surviving placement ID is insufficient for fit reuse. Its full transform, source revision, preparation revision, relevant obstacles, fit settings and evaluation-time policy must still be compatible. Density, quota, Relax and acceptance changes may alter surviving placement inputs under the current foundation.

Use the asset audit template on a representative set: clean ribbons, disconnected blades, joined-base clumps, precurved blades, opacity cards, broad leaves, scans and proxy sources. Record manual preparation time and rejection causes. Do not turn an untested “usually automatic” claim into an asset support promise.

04 Collision and solvers

Status: inspected dependency behavior, supplied diagnostics, engineering hypotheses and a comparison plan. The R12 source review, R13 review and R14 review distinguish code checks, reported native runs and analytic fixtures. No production solver has been selected or independently executed here.

Neighborhoods and geometry roles

Keep render source, driver geometry, receiver and obstacle proxy distinct. A receiver anchors a plant; it is not automatically an obstacle. Rooted mode uses one-way interaction initially: the fixed stone affects the grass, and the stone receives no reaction motion. Plant-to-plant contact is a separate opt-in scope.

Build a bounded spatial broad phase over accepted plant reach and obstacle geometry. Query potential contacts before narrow-phase tests. Use the union of old/new obstacle influence and previous dependencies during updates. An obstacle moving away matters as much as one moving into contact. A single nearest object or nearest surface sample cannot establish the complete constraint set.

Contact fidelity

The collision representation must match the intended behavior. A convex hull can remove concavities; a terrain hull can bridge a valley; a conservative capsule can block a ribbon that could physically turn edge-on through a gap. Show proxies and their effective margins.

For a thin blade, test whole segments/capsules or an explicitly represented ribbon. Endpoint tests can miss a segment crossing a stone. Finite surface samples can miss crossings between samples. A component fully inside a closed obstacle can require inside/outside classification even when no triangle pair intersects.

After decoding, validate the visible mesh against every relevant obstacle. Guide clearance does not prove mesh-edge or triangle clearance. Smoothing, shell thickness, interpolation and rendering subdivision can introduce new contact. The validation envelope must match the geometry actually delivered.

For signed distance fields, record voxel size, band coverage, sign validity and approximation error. Open meshes, thin surfaces and intersecting obstacles need a declared policy. A narrow polygon gap can disappear in a coarse field. Surface normals alone do not supply robust inside/outside semantics for arbitrary meshes.

Clearance accounting

Define terms once: visible half-width or proxy radius, requested clearance, obstacle padding, discretization error and numerical acceptance tolerance. Avoid counting the same width twice. For a straight parallel channel, if effective radius already includes visible half-width plus clearance, a necessary centerline-channel width is approximately twice that radius; it is not that value plus the blade width again.

That lower bound is not a general feasibility proof. Curvature, base attachment, admissible strain and full three-dimensional routes matter. Opposing contacts must remain separate constraints; averaging their normals can cancel the information needed to escape penetration.

What R12 resolves at the Jolt pin

The local Lab build pins commit 1b2e77e3c6c09709e5edc79dc208459b8ff32051; its Core header says 5.6.1. Eleven decision-critical local files match the public files at that commit after line-ending normalization. This is a source check, not a built/loaded-module identity check or an audit of the complete dependency. The exact commit and build flags matter more than a release nickname.

R13's wider source ledger matches 48 local file hashes, while the pinned CMake project string remains 5.6.0. Preserve both observations rather than substituting a nearby tag. Reported build flags and binary/loaded identity still need their own execution evidence.

In the checked bare CPU rod environmental path, shapes receive vertex positions and inverse masses and keep one selected collision plane per vertex. That interface supplies no rod segment or varying blade-width envelope. A segment can cross a stone while both sampled endpoints remain outside. Adding samples can reduce this error but does not prove continuous whole-segment contact. This finding is limited to that path: the engine also has soft-body face handling in its rigid-body CCD path, so it must not be generalized to every Jolt soft-body subsystem. Vertex iterator, rigid-body CCD implementation.

Root construction also needs a controlled test. The normal rod-property calculation uses the minimum endpoint inverse mass for a rod, and its angular corrections use that rod inverse mass. A zero-inverse-mass root can therefore suppress adjacent rod rotation corrections and behave more like a clamp than an intended hinge. For a relaxation method, refine timestep/iterations and discretization separately. Measure residual motion, strain and contact error; reaching a chosen frame is not convergence. Record whether results depend on initialization, obstacle approach order or previous fitted state.

Gate 3: lifecycle and product behavior

Move an obstacle into contact, across the vegetation, away from contact, then delete it. Include old/new influence and released plants. Compare recomputation with a fresh setup using the same declared rest/initial state.

Exercise Manual pending edits, relevant Live edits/animation, unrelated animation, selection/navigation, owner switching, cancel, stale completion, deletion, scene reset, save/reopen and Undo/Redo. Check that failures retain the prior complete publication and that its status honestly reports staleness.

Move an obstacle between repeated queries while a job runs: that job must keep one coherent geometry/pose generation even though its successor becomes stale. Also exercise rigid and fitting services starting/stopping together under the actual shared-runtime/module ownership. A safe individual query or a final revision check does not prove either lifecycle contract.

Test placement-input changes: source swap, density/quota, masks, Relax, transform edits, relevant source topology and appearance-only changes. Reuse should follow compatible full inputs, not stable IDs alone.

Gate 4: bounded scale and memory

Measure cold preparation, warm update and no-op reuse separately. Scale affected and unaffected populations independently; a mostly unaffected field must not pay full deformation/output cost. Increase contact density and source vertex count independently of placement count.

Capture neighborhood candidates, active contacts, guide samples, solve work, decoded vertices, output variants, cache reuse, uploads, wall time and actual responsiveness. Account for retained source data, staged/published overlap, render copies and Undo/Redo history. Test allocation/budget failure and resource retirement.

Add unit/local-origin invariance, engine error-bit handling, job/barrier safety and cancellation-latency measurements before accepting engine reuse. The current Lab declares a 5,000-body default/admitted profile. R12's proposed 10,000-body comparison exceeds that profile and requires a separately scoped harness/admission decision; this research update changes no limits or settings.

A GPU or approximate variant path is justified only if these measurements identify a bottleneck and the proposed optimization preserves acceptance. Report absolute memory and complete-pipeline timings with hardware/build/input identity.
