CYRUSSCATTER / ENGINEERING DECISION REPORT

1. Executive recommendation

Proceed with a bounded research prototype for static, rooted vegetation contact fitting. Do not yet commit to a general vegetation physics system or an arbitrary-mesh deformation promise.

The useful first feature is an optional deformation stage after placements have been accepted. Prepared grass blades retain their roots and instance identities while their shapes are adjusted around selected fixed scene objects or fixed objects in another Scatter layer. Results are validated, cached, and published as a complete generation shared by viewport, rendering, and Bake.

Use a small CPU implementation with an explicit asset contract and a canonical mesh evaluator. Compare three numerical candidates through the same input/output path:

  1. P0 — Static guide fitting: a length-preserving, root-fixed procedural initialization, followed by bounded contact-aware refinement of guide directions.
  2. P1 — XPBD guides: stretch, rest-bend and root constraints with external contact, used to produce a stored pose.
  3. P2 — Jolt CPU rods: a separate comparator if the actual Physics Lab dependency version and build can be identified and qualified.

P0 is the preferred starting baseline, not a proven winner. If it meets the visual and contact requirements more simply, keep it. If XPBD or Jolt passes substantially more of the required cases at acceptable total cost, choose that evidence instead. A small static constrained problem can still require iterations and robust contact detection; avoiding dynamics does not make competing constraints disappear.

1.1 What the evidence changes

A full physical simulation is unnecessary for the initial promise. SideFX documents a Guide Collide With VDB operation that fits guide curves against distance fields, separately from its Vellum simulation workflow. This establishes a relevant procedural workflow precedent, not the implementation or guarantees of a prospective Cyrus feature. H01

Current Jolt deserves a real comparison. CPU Cosserat rod constraints were added in Jolt 5.4.0, including a vegetation example. Jolt 5.6.0 also adds a separate, experimental GPU hair path whose environment collisions are limited to convex hulls and compound shapes. Neither establishes what is bundled in Physics Lab or solves Scatter integration. J01 J02

Geometry delivery is a first-order risk. Max 2027 render-time instancing groups targets around a shared source Mesh or INode. Arbitrary different bends are different geometry unless the consumer evaluates a supported deformation representation. Compact guide storage does not by itself preserve renderer geometry sharing. There is no universal asset conversion. Imported meshes may be usable, but the system needs roots, meaningful deformable pieces, sufficient segmentation, and reliable vertex binding. Opacity artwork and dense scans do not provide this anatomical structure automatically.

2. Precise feature definition and initial limits

2.1 Proposed feature: Rooted Contact Fit

Given an already accepted vegetation instance, its prepared rest geometry, root constraints, and a set of fixed obstacle snapshots, find a visually plausible shape that reduces intersections while respecting declared root, length, bend and clearance limits.

The result is a static authored pose. It is not a placement filter. Enabling, changing, or resetting contact fitting must not change the distribution seed, create different placement identities, move roots, or silently delete accepted instances.

Ordinary scene objects and other-layer objects use the same collision-query interface after snapshotting. A scatter-produced stone has two identities: its source geometry identity and its stable obstacle-layer placement identity. Its center alone is not its collision shape.

2.2 Honest claims

Mechanical bending Elastic response to loads and contact, with a specified rest shape and constitutive model Only if validated: a rod/static-equilibrium option may be described as physically based within its assumptions

A plant forced around a pre-existing stone is not necessarily the shape produced by physically lowering the stone into the plant. Friction, trapping and loading history can change the result. A static solver also cannot infer that grass should never have grown beneath a rock.

2.3 First-release boundary

Support prepared, segmented, nonbranching blades or clumps of such blades; fixed roots; one-way interaction with static obstacles; positive uniform instance scale; exact CPU output for affected plants; and a bounded, explicit Update/Fit workflow.

Include multiple obstacles, irregular closed rocks, and diagnosis of impossible contacts. Initially require explicit collision proxies or sheet semantics for ambiguous open/nonmanifold obstacles. Unsupported sources remain visible and undeformed with an explanation.

Defer plant–plant interaction, branching mechanics, broad-leaf crumpling, moving roots, continuous wind, animated/deforming colliders, two-way stone motion, automatic biological reconstruction, and arbitrary renderer-only proxy deformation.

Time policy: Fit samples the scene at a recorded evaluation frame. Scrubbing does not resimulate or silently substitute animated source, receiver or placement state. A control labeled “Fit at current frame” changes that sample. Live mode reacts to relevant authoring edits evaluated at that sample. Time-dependent behavior is a separate later contract.

3.1 Proximity deformation: useful baseline, insufficient guarantee

For rest centerline position x0(s)x_0(s), a simple rule is

x(s)=x0(s)+w(s)d(x0(s)),w(0)=0.x(s)=x_0(s)+w(s)d(x_0(s)),\qquad w(0)=0.

Here dd is an obstacle-derived displacement, and ww controls influence along the plant. Requiring w′(0)=0w'(0)=0 helps preserve the root tangent for a smooth field. A height mask is reasonable for a consistently oriented single plant; it is not equivalent to each blade’s arc coordinate.

This method can reduce visible intersections without physics. However:

  • Offset vectors alter lengths and can stretch the mesh.
  • Nearest-surface normals can jump at edges and medial axes.
  • Summed obstacle repulsion can cancel in a narrow gap.
  • A locally outward direction may lead into another obstacle.
  • Repeated projection and smoothing can shrink, stretch or oscillate.
  • Overhangs and cavities require a route around geometry, not simply an outward vector.
  • A distant root may have a long blade already touching the object.

SideFX’s Guide Process explicitly provides per-segment rotation and bend operations. It is a useful authoring precedent; its private internals are not evidence for a specific Cyrus algorithm. H02

3.2 A stronger procedural baseline

Represent a blade by a pinned root rr, rest lengths ℓj\ell_j, and segment frames RjR_j:

x0=r,xj+1=xj+ℓjRjez.x_0=r,\qquad x_{j+1}=x_j+\ell_jR_j e_z.

Provided RjR_j is a valid rotation, each guide segment keeps its length. The first tangent or frame may be clamped, or allowed a declared base flexibility. A root-position pin alone permits swiveling of the whole blade.

Start with one or two distributed bend controls for simple rocks. Increase to independent segment rotations only when the visual/contact test demonstrates the need. Preserve the original curved rest configuration; do not make “shape preservation” mean straightening.

Changing that control basis changes the feasible deformation space. Record it in preprocessing/fit keys and compare it separately from an iteration or time budget.

subject to roots, guide length, maximum bend, and obstacle clearance constraints. Here κ\kappa and τ\tau are consistently defined discrete rest-relative bend/twist measures. Scale or normalize weights explicitly; arbitrary artist parameters are not automatically elastic moduli.

One concrete convention uses the rotation logarithm of the change from each rest relative material-frame rotation to its fitted counterpart. Resolve quaternion signs/logarithm branches consistently, divide by the associated rest arc length, and separate bend from twist in the declared material axes. Scalar bend angles alone cannot preserve a three-dimensional rest shape.

A practical prototype can use bounded coordinate updates or projected optimization with a contact penalty

Ec=wc∑kmin(0,gk)2,E_c=w_c\sum_k \min(0,g_k)^2,

where gkg_k is the clearance residual. Penalty minimization permits penetration; it must be followed by independent validation. An explicit feasibility phase or constrained solve may improve this but does not guarantee a solution to an infeasible scene. Strong pointwise rest attraction can also oppose natural sliding; prioritize rest curvature over unnecessarily pinning the entire blade in space.

This is a geometric fitting problem. It is not a claim that a general optimizer will beat XPBD in complexity or runtime.

3.3 XPBD: reasons to test it, reasons not to assume it wins

XPBD introduces compliance and accumulated constraint multipliers to address PBD’s timestep/iteration-dependent stiffness. The standard scalar update is: P01

α̃=α/Δt2,Δλ=−C(x)−α̃λ∑iwi∥∇iC∥2+α̃,\tilde\alpha=\alpha/\Delta t^2,\qquad \Delta\lambda= \frac{-C(x)-\tilde\alpha\lambda} {\sum_i w_i\|\nabla_i C\|^2+\tilde\alpha},

Δxi=wi∇iCΔλ,λ←λ+Δλ.\Delta x_i=w_i\nabla_i C\,\Delta\lambda,\qquad \lambda\leftarrow\lambda+\Delta\lambda.

For contact, the multiplier and update must respect the inequality’s sign convention and unilateral active set. Equality formulas cannot simply be copied without this treatment.

Initialize multipliers from a declared state for each timestep/fit, accumulate them within that solve, and explicitly specify any cross-step or cross-fit warm start.

Use zero inverse mass for pinned positional degrees of freedom. Root orientation needs an additional tangent/frame constraint. Rest bend angles or material frames preserve the source shape; distance constraints alone do not. Position-only chains have no independent material twist.

A finite solve still has convergence error, ordering effects, incomplete contacts and different local minima. The Small Steps paper explicitly investigates how substeps and iterations alter results; XPBD should not be marketed as insensitive to all work-budget choices. P02

For static art direction, dynamic settling adds mass, damping, timestep and stop conditions that may have no user value. Compare at equal final visual/contact quality, not equal iteration counts. Reset from the same rest pose for comparison; an undocumented previous-pose warm start introduces history dependence. Warm starts may be a later speed optimization only if their result difference is measured.

3.4 Rods, shells and alternatives

Discrete Elastic Rods provides a framed-curve mechanical treatment with rest geometry, bending/twist coupling and inextensibility. Position and Orientation Based Cosserat Rods offers a position/orientation constraint model. These are relevant when cross-section direction and twist matter. Neither paper supplies automatic imported-plant interpretation or Max presentation. P03 P04

If local iterations become inefficient for stiff long guides, a direct stiff-rod/tree solve and the newer Stable Cosserat Rods formulation are credible alternatives. Their existence is a reason to keep the test interface small and solver-neutral, not to build a general framework in advance. P05 P06

Thin shell models are useful for a broad leaf whose two sides curl differently. A centerline cannot express those degrees of freedom. Discrete Shells explicitly addresses thin structures with curved rest shapes. A four-vertex alpha card still needs subdivision before it can fold convincingly. P07

General volumetric simulation is poorly matched to narrow blades: it adds interior meshing, volume behavior and state that the visual goal rarely needs. Retain it only as a future possibility for thick succulent leaves or other soft objects.

3.5 Reusable variants and hybrids

A variant is a shared deformed source mesh plus ordinary instance transforms. Quantize bend direction, bend magnitude, contact height and source scale only as far as the resulting error budget allows.

Selection must be followed by per-placement validation against the actual obstacles. Two similar guide poses may differ at the exact contact point; a visually small variant error can reintroduce penetration. Let an instance fall back to a unique shape when no variant passes.

Cache keys include source/preprocessing revision, evaluator version, quantized state, transform class and shading layout. A capped cache may evict only unreferenced variants. If KK approaches AA, sharing disappears. Material differences can multiply render submissions even when geometry is shared.

For static Fit, discrete shape changes on the next explicit update are understandable. In Live mode, thresholds can create jumps. Hysteresis reduces switching but adds history dependence; interpolation can violate clearance. Do not add temporal interpolation without revalidation.

The strongest initial hybrid is existing transform-only output for unaffected plants plus exact CPU-deformed output near contact. Variants are an optimization to earn through measurements.

4.2 Root and guide identification

A pivot/local-height mask is sufficient for a coarse artistic bend of a consistently upright coherent object. It can also identify candidate roots on clean strip-like blades with a reliable growth axis.

It fails for curled or hanging leaves, blades whose tips return near the ground, several root elevations, sloped planting, inconsistent pivots and cards containing many painted plants. Neither a pivot nor vertex count establishes the growth direction.

A bounded automatic assistant may:

  1. Find logical connected components while preserving render seams.
  2. Use an artist-selected growth axis or root region to propose root candidates.
  3. Recognize a strip/tube topology, trace its centerline and resample by arc length.
  4. Display roots, guide direction and confidence for approval.
  5. Refuse ambiguous branches, fused blades and non-strip components.

Do not indiscriminately weld close vertices: separate blades can touch in space. UV or normal seams may split a single blade into apparent components. Mesh inference therefore needs logical connectivity distinct from the final render indexing.

Authored root groups, vertex colors or curves are useful when their semantics are explicit. A random vertex-color channel cannot be assumed to mean stiffness. Bones are an optional preparation route, not a requirement for every source.

SideFX Vellum Hair distinguishes root position from orientation constraints, and explicitly treats its basic hair model as unbranching, with splitting/welding for branched input. That is a useful preparation precedent, not proof of a general plant extractor. H03

4.3 Binding visible geometry to reduced guides

For a render vertex vv assigned to guide gg, store rest arc coordinate ss, a segment/interpolation coordinate, and cross-section offset

ov=Rg0(s)T(v−cg0(s)).o_v=R_g^0(s)^T\left(v-c_g^0(s)\right).

The proposed deformation is

v′=cg(s)+Rg(s)ov.v'=c_g(s)+R_g(s)o_v.

Capture is restricted to the blade/component ID. Nearest-guide binding across interleaved blades causes cross-talk. A root ring can be held exactly fixed, with a smooth transition above it. Multiple-guide weights require explicit piece/junction rules and normalization.

Use rotation-minimizing transport or another documented stable material-frame rule; a Frenet frame is undefined at zero curvature and can flip. Preserve authored twist where present. Detect zero-length segments, nearly reversed directions and inconsistent quaternion signs. The RMF literature supplies a relevant frame-construction reference. P08

This mapping does not make the visible surface inextensible. The outside of a wide bend can stretch even when the centerline length is exact. Measure mesh edge/area strain, folding and contacts separately. An ordinary rotation can reverse a triangle normal relative to its world-space rest normal; that alone is not an inversion test. Use transported local orientation, nondegenerate area and intersection checks.

SideFX Wire Deform documents rest/deformed capture, frame behavior and normal handling; it also warns that a coordinate-only cache assumption becomes invalid when topology or attributes change. H04

4.4 Segmentation, shading and opacity

For a locally circular bend with curvature κ\kappa and segment length ℓ\ell, small-angle chord error is approximately

echord≈κℓ2/8.e_{\text{chord}}\approx\kappa\ell^2/8.

A proposed preparation criterion is ℓ≤8ϵshape/κmax\ell\leq\sqrt{8\epsilon_{\text{shape}}/\kappa_{\max}}, together with a maximum turn per segment. For a 0.10 m bend radius and 0.025 m segments, the estimate is about 0.78 mm. This is geometry arithmetic, not a visual-quality measurement. A sharp rock edge still needs a finite bend radius.

Increasing guide samples does not add visible mesh vertices. Non-destructive preprocessing subdivision can be offered explicitly, but it creates new topology, binding, memory and export obligations.

Preserve UV coordinates and UV-face indexing, face material IDs, vertex colors, smoothing groups and split-normal identity. Recompute geometric normals/bounds from final positions. For authored normals, choose a documented transport rule or a nonsingular deformation-Jacobian inverse transpose; blindly regenerating smooth normals can destroy deliberately edited grass shading. Tangents must remain consistent with UVs, deformed positions and handedness.

Opacity affects appearance, not the geometric collision boundary. A full rectangle is conservative but can push transparent card regions away unnecessarily. Alpha-aware collision would require texture access, threshold/filter semantics, UV interpretation, resolution and invalidation. Defer it initially. Label the mode “card fitting” and test silhouettes/shadows separately from mesh geometry.

Post-solve smoothing or subdivision must precede final contact validation. SideFX explicitly documents that these operations can reintroduce collisions and that independent corrective point moves can harm appearance. H05

4.5 Scale, mirroring, shear and invalidation

Convert Max system units into a declared solver unit, preferably meters. Root anchoring and clearances must use physical scale, not display-unit labels. Rebase solve regions near the plant and use double precision for snapshot/query arithmetic where needed. Rebasing cannot recover detail already lost in a float world-coordinate conversion.

For positive uniform scale, rest lengths, blade width, offsets and collision thickness scale coherently. An artistic “flexibility” can be scale-normalized; a physical bending stiffness follows a material/cross-section model and cannot use that same rule without explanation.

For nonuniform scale or shear, transform the rest geometry fully and recompute rest lengths/frames in the solve metric. A local sphere becomes an ellipsoid; multiplying local SDF distances by one scale is generally wrong. Mirroring changes orientation and tangent handedness. Qualify winding, one/two-sided materials and normals explicitly. Reject singular transforms.

Invalidate preprocessing/bindings on changes to topology, vertex order, rest positions, logical blade IDs, roots, guides, pivot/growth convention, source object-space transform, subdivision or binding algorithm version. A same-count reordered mesh is still a different binding.

UV/material-only edits normally invalidate shading/output caches, not the guide solve, unless collision geometry was derived from alpha/displacement. A source rest-position edit invalidates the solve even when topology is unchanged. Renderer displacement and any downstream geometry modifier must either be represented in the collision contract or explicitly excluded.

4.6 Broad phase: select reachable geometry, not nearest centers

Use a spatial index over plant reach envelopes and obstacle-instance bounds. A BVH handles mixed sizes without a mandatory uniform cell size; a grid/hash may be simpler for regular grass populations, but long objects occupy many cells and dense buckets can degenerate.

For maximum permitted blade length Lmax=(1+ϵstretch,max)LrestL_{\max}=(1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{stretch,max}})L_{\mathrm{rest}}, a conservative contact-reach sphere is

Rreach=Lmax+rmax+c+ϵbudget,R_{\text{reach}}=L_{\max}+r_{\max}+c+\epsilon_{\text{budget}},

where rmaxr_{\max} bounds the maximum binding-offset norm and cc is clearance. Include binding/chord uncertainty in the budget. Expand the query by the extra artistic influence/push range when that mode can shape plants without contact. A tighter bound is valid only if the fit’s permitted displacement/bend range proves it conservative.

Rest bounds alone can miss geometry reached after bending. Include prior deformed bounds for update discovery and either the full reach envelope or swept/current bounds with candidate refresh. Influence distance is an artistic falloff in addition to contact discovery, not a root-center cutoff.

Collect every relevant obstacle. Use a top-level index for scene/obstacle instances and reusable source-local triangle BVHs for rigid geometry. Another Scatter layer can provide many transforms referencing one shared rock collision mesh.

If a stone moves, query the union of its old/new bounds, the plants previously depending on it, and the reach index. Plants that stop touching must be refitted or restored to rest on the next update. No longer intersecting the new obstacle bounds does not make their old deformation valid.

4.7 Narrow phase and contact semantics

The initial reference path should use analytic spheres/boxes where authored and triangle BVHs elsewhere, with explicit segment/capsule contact. CGAL’s AABB manual documents segment intersection queries, but its stock distance interface is point-based; citing the library is not proof of a ready capsule-distance solution. C01

A simple falsification case is a segment from (−2,0,0)(-2,0,0) to (2,0,0)(2,0,0) through a unit sphere. Both endpoints are outside; the segment crosses the sphere. Sparse point-only tests miss it.

For each guide edge, check segment–triangle distance against its envelope radius plus clearance, and detect crossings. A segment wholly inside a closed solid also needs inside/outside classification. At a zero-distance crossing, the separation vector supplies no unique normal: use geometric features/manifolds, a known feasible side, or an explicit recovery preference. Report ambiguity instead of normalizing a zero vector.

A centerline capsule with half-width radius may be overly conservative for flat ribbons. Compare that baseline with a thin ribbon collision proxy. In either case, validate the final deformed render triangles. A clear centerline does not certify the leaf edges.

Half-width alone also does not prove that a capsule encloses triangles across guide joints or interpolated frames. Derive the envelope from the binding/proxy or add a justified chord/binding margin. Treat an unproven capsule as a fitting approximation.

64T+12T+12Vobytes.64T+12T+12V_o\ \text{bytes}.

At T=106T=10^6 and Vo=0.5×106V_o=0.5\times10^6, these arrays total about 82 MB decimal / 78.2 MiB, excluding scratch, metadata and extra caches. This is not CGAL’s measured allocation.

A useful analytical check: an exact distance field is 1-Lipschitz. Samples spaced at most hh along a straight segment, all above required clearance by h/2h/2, bound the unsampled distance. An interpolated SDF needs an additional verified field-error bound. Without that bound, sampling remains a heuristic.

Recommendation: BVH first; add an SDF only if measured repeated-query cost justifies build time, memory and a verified error budget. Keep geometric validation even if an SDF supplies the fit direction.

4.9 Independence and coupling

With fixed obstacles, no plant–plant contact, no shared cage and no branch junctions, different blades can be solved independently. Several stones still couple the constraints within each blade.

A shared clump cage, plant–plant collision, a branch graph or moving obstacle response creates coupled islands. Influence can propagate beyond the original selection. Vegetation-to-vegetation contact also changes the candidate-pair count from vegetation-versus-fixed-obstacles to a potentially dense deformable contact graph. Treat it as a separate future problem.

5. Recommended architecture and data flow

5.1 Integrate after accepted placement

The ordering is:

placement generation → projection/spacing/acceptance/Relax → accepted placement snapshot → contact fitting → final validation → presentation.

Contact fitting consumes accepted placements. The receiving ground is not automatically a collider: if it is selected, the authored attachment region needs an explicit ground-contact exemption; that exemption must not suppress stone contacts. Contact fitting does not reuse the acceptance/rejection machinery to remove vegetation. An upstream placement edit invalidates corresponding deformation; a downstream cleanup/Relax step must not silently move the fitted roots.

If future placement rules deliberately avoid roots beneath rocks, expose that as a separate placement decision. Its changed population is not a successful deformation result.

Proposed data flow. Host interaction occurs at snapshot/preparation/publication boundaries. Workers consume owned numerical data. The failure path keeps the previous complete generation.

5.4 Cache keys and precise invalidation

A representative source key is:

Ks=(source ID,rtopo,rgeom,rroots,rguides,rbinding,rprepare,units).K_s=(\text{source ID},r_{\mathrm{topo}},r_{\mathrm{geom}},r_{\mathrm{roots}}, r_{\mathrm{guides}},r_{\mathrm{binding}},r_{\mathrm{prepare}},\text{units}).

A fit key includes stable placement ID, source key, placement transform/receiver revision, obstacle identities and relevant geometry/transform revisions, fitting settings, sample time, solver/evaluator version and precision policy.

A placement-set change is not automatically a reason to recompute every surviving plant. Retain a surviving result only if its source, transform, receiver and obstacle dependencies are compatible. Added IDs are prepared; removed IDs disappear because the upstream population changed, not because contact failed.

Maintain geometric and shading revisions separately. Material selection, camera movement, selection highlighting and UI browsing must not dirty the numerical fit. Source map-channel changes may rebuild render streams without changing guide positions. Opacity/displacement changes matter if used by the declared collision representation.

An obstacle change can affect plants that previously had no contact. Reverse dependency records alone are insufficient; combine them with reach-index queries over changed bounds. An obstacle deletion also affects plants formerly bent around it.

5.5 Manual, Live and complete generations

In Manual, pending edits do not run source preprocessing, obstacle builds, fits or geometry uploads merely to present a new fit. Reuse the last complete generation. UI diagnostics must say that it was fitted against earlier revisions.

Moving the actual scene stone still moves that stone; the old grass result may now intersect it. “Preserved last result” is not “still contact-valid against the current scene.”

In Live, coalesce relevant authoring edits, snapshot on the host thread, and allow one successor job per affected publication group. Cancel or invalidate older work. Compare captured dependency values before recomputing when notifications are broad. Browsing, selecting, camera movement and unrelated animation should show zero fit/snapshot/mesh-upload increments.

Keep placement/source snapshots compatible across a publication. In Manual, a changed source topology must not silently feed old bindings. The current generation owns the geometry it needs; pending source geometry is used only by the successor.

Live quality may finish later than a drag. It is acceptable to show the completed previous generation plus “pending,” rather than publish half the instances.

5.6 Other Scatter layers and cycles

For v1, obstacle layers contain fixed, nondeforming accepted instances. Snapshot their geometry source references and transforms once per relevant revision. Do not create a Max scene node for every obstacle instance solely for collision.

A foliage layer can depend on a stone layer. A changed stone-layer source invalidates its BLAS; changed transforms/placements update the top-level index and affected regions.

Reject self-dependencies and cycles at authoring time. A small traversal of the existing layer relationship list is enough; no generic graph framework is justified.

Future deformed obstacle layers would need the published deformed geometry, not an undeformed approximation presented as exact contact. They also need a topological publication schedule or explicitly frozen external generations. Simultaneous mutual fitting is a coupled solve and is outside this feature.

5.7 Atomicity, resource lifetime and failure

A generation is publishable only after numerical validation and all required plugin-owned CPU/native/display resources and render descriptors are ready. Renderer-owned BVHs and translation may be allocated later by the renderer; do not claim those were atomically prebuilt.

An in-flight render leases generation G while the viewport may move to G+1. This is valid. Parity comparisons use the same generation, not necessarily the same wall-clock moment.

Readers hold immutable geometry. Host-owned destruction is queued to the host thread after CPU leases end; GPU buffers also require safe completion/fence handling. Prepare native lazy caches such as normals/bounds before exposing them, and test whether any consumer still mutates returned data.

Old generations must own usable geometry, not raw source-node pointers that deletion can invalidate. Material/texture appearance is a separate policy: retain safe host references, prepare renderer material access on the host, and allow ordinary material edits without a numerical refit when geometry is unaffected. Do not describe the geometry snapshot as a frozen copy of all external texture files.

Cancellation, out-of-memory, device failure, invalid binding, missing dependencies or a stale token discard the successor. The previous complete result remains. Do not evict leased buffers to satisfy a new job.

5.8 Undo/Redo, save/load and reset

Undo/Redo proposal: authored edits create normal host Undo records. A Fit completion creates one result-publication record only if its request token still matches the current authoring/Undo epoch. Undo, reset, load, deletion or intervening edits invalidate obsolete completions. Live-derived updates should be associated with the initiating authoring state or a clearly specified result policy; do not hold a Max Undo transaction open throughout worker execution.

Undo restores the compatible authored state and generation reference. Redo restores the corresponding completed generation where retained. If history buffers have been explicitly pruned, rebuilding numeric output from stored guides/bindings is allowed; silently resolving to a different physical pose is not.

Save proposal: store one committed generation’s versioned source/binding/rest geometry, sparse guide states or variants, stable placement correspondence, evaluation frame, relevant input fingerprints and diagnostics, plus authored pending settings and a pending/stale flag. Sharing/compression is permitted; GPU resources, raw pointers and an opaque engine-world blob are not the persistence format.

Save/reopen may rebuild derived host/display resources from saved numeric data. It must not require a fresh fit, silently apply old bindings to new topology, or lose Manual pending state. Missing source/obstacle dependencies leave the frozen result available where saved geometry suffices, with a stale/missing label. Missing materials/textures are reported separately.

Reset deformation is an explicit undoable authoring command producing a compatible rest-output generation. It removes sparse shape records, not placements. A failed fit must never call Reset automatically.

6.4 Proxies, normals, bounds and picking

A proxy’s viewport representation may be a box, points or a reduced mesh. Full geometry must be verified through a supported extraction path before deformation. Corona documents that Full Mesh remains loaded in RAM; V-Ray documents use of full-mesh display with modifiers. That path warrants a separate memory test. Proxy conversion should be an explicit preparation action with an estimate. R08 R09

Bounds include the final render mesh, width and any declared downstream expansion. Guide-point bounds alone can incorrectly cull leaf edges. Picking must query committed deformed geometry or a declared bounded approximation and return stable placement identity.

Preserve UVs, materials and authored-normal intent as specified in Section 4. A two-sided leaf cannot be fixed by ignoring winding changes under mirroring. Handle geometry and shading parity separately.

6.5 Parity and Bake contract

For a given generation, the viewport’s full-quality geometry, render geometry and Bake must use the same evaluation/version and transform convention. A reduced preview mode must be labeled and have a declared approximation bound.

A proposed exact-path tolerance is

ϵv=max(0.01 mm,10−5L),\epsilon_v=\max(0.01\text{ mm},10^{-5}L),

with maximum matching-vertex position error below ϵv\epsilon_v and normal angular error below 0.1° where identical normals are intended. These are proposed test thresholds, not certified guarantees. Reject scales/coordinates for which they are unrepresentable.

Quantized variants and export conversions need their own measured error limits, included in the total clearance margin. Compare geometry/depth/silhouette with a fixed camera; beauty-render color is confounded by lighting, material filtering and noise.

Bake materializes the committed mesh, map channels, material assignments, transform and stable-ID metadata where the target format supports it. Reimport may reorder/split vertices, so compare geometric surfaces and attribute correspondence rather than raw array order only.

Unbounded renderer displacement, renderer subdivision that moves the surface, or unmodeled post-modifiers invalidate the contact claim. Initially exclude these or require a verified maximum expansion envelope.

The default panel should answer five practical questions: which plants, which obstacles, how flexible, how much clearance, and when to update?

10. Bounded prototype plan and go/no-go gates

This section proposes experiments; none have been run. The prototype is an evaluation harness, not an instruction to merge production code.

Gate 0 — Prove the output path before solver investment

Input: one prepared 0.20 m blade with 8 longitudinal segments, two UV channels, two material IDs and explicit normals. Three placements use fixed straight, 30° and 70° guide bends; include an unaffected fourth placement. Replicate the setup to expose accidental geometry sharing.

Observe: Max 2027 viewport, Corona, Arnold, V-Ray, Bake/reimport; final bounds/picking; source immutability; stable IDs; actual renderer callbacks/threads; CPU/native/GPU/renderer allocations.

Go: required production render paths receive distinct correct geometry within the declared tolerance, without source mutation or an unbudgeted all-N expansion. No-go: only the viewport bends, one instance overwrites another, callbacks evaluate the host off-thread, or output lifetimes are unsafe.

If one renderer cannot be qualified, decide a narrower supported renderer scope explicitly. Do not let a solver prototype disguise a missing product output path.

Gate 1 — Establish the asset contract

Input: at least six prepared fixtures: straight blade, curved blade, disconnected clump, segmented alpha card, deliberately undersegmented card, and a dense scan that should be rejected or require authored preparation.

Observe: preparation time, ambiguity count, binding errors, artist steps and preserved shading. Include a real commercial grass source with appropriate permission, not only clean synthetic geometry.

Go: prepared narrow blades/clumps bind reliably and unsupported assets receive precise instructions. No-go for a broad import promise: automatic inference silently merges blades, guesses the wrong roots or requires extensive repair on representative assets.

Decision: if manual preparation is dominant, a source-preparation tool and documented supported library become prerequisites to product release.

Gate 2 — Compare only three numerical candidates initially

Use P0 static fitting, P1 XPBD and conditional P2 Jolt CPU rods. Share rest geometry, root settings, contact representation, final mesh evaluator and validator. Compare one blade against sphere/box/irregular rock, one clump, two competing stones and initial interpenetration.

Go: one candidate passes the feasible fixture suite with acceptable visuals and bounded cost. No-go: it reports success while changing roots, deleting plants, missing segment crossings or exceeding strain/bend limits.

If P0 passes, stop solver expansion. If it fails multi-contact cases but P1/P2 passes, compare total complexity/performance. If all fail because the render binding folds, fix the asset/output representation before trying a fourth solver.

Only escalate to direct stiff-rod or Stable Cosserat research when measured convergence identifies a concrete need.

Gate 3 — Qualify lifecycle and dependencies

Input: automated host scenarios for Manual/Live, relevant/unrelated edits, source topology changes, obstacle deletion, Undo/Redo, save/reopen, cancellation, stale completion, memory fault injection and concurrent renders.

Go: every completed consumer session sees one compatible generation; stale/failed jobs never replace it; pending state survives save/reopen; counters show no numerical work from unrelated interaction.

No-go: any dangling resource, mixed binding/source revision, surprise Undo result, hidden solve on reopening, or scene access from workers.

This gate also checks that source geometry is frozen while material/texture references follow the declared separate policy.

Gate 4 — Measure scale and choose optimization

Run Section 7’s 100/1,000/10,000 affected-clump scenarios and large mostly unaffected populations. First measure exact CPU output; then compare batching and variants only where a bottleneck exists.

Proposed provisional product budgets, to be fixed against a named baseline machine before the run:

  • For the 1,000-clump baseline (G = 12, S = 8, V = 1,000, 100k obstacle triangles), end-to-end warm Fit p95 ≤ 10 seconds and additional peak host memory ≤ 1 GiB.
  • Cooperative cancel response ≤ 250 ms for bounded worker work; separately disclose any noninterruptible host snapshot/library call.
  • Host publication and staging slices should avoid a >50 ms UI stall target; measure total host work separately.
  • Panning/selection/unrelated animation: zero fit invocations and zero deformation-buffer reuploads.
  • 10,000 affected clumps are a stress/architecture test, not an initial capacity commitment.

These are suggested acceptance budgets, not predictions. Revise them with product requirements before comparison, not after seeing a favored solver’s result.

Go: a supported scope fits agreed budgets; incremental work follows changed/affected geometry. No-go for Live mode: repeated relevant edits create unbounded backlog, allocation growth or unacceptable stalls. A useful Manual-only release remains an explicit alternative.

Gate 5 — Visual review and failure usability

Use fixed close-up and medium-distance cameras with neutral clay, final material, silhouette and contact-debug views. Compare P0/P1/P2 blind where practical. Ask artists to rate natural curvature, root planting, local flattening, clump diversity, edge clipping and visible repetition.

Measure the frequency of infeasible roots in realistic scatters. If a single blocked root routinely prevents useful work, strict whole-job rejection is insufficient as the only product behavior.

Required refinement if frequent: an explicit “Use successful fits; retain unresolved plants” action publishes one complete generation with compatible prior/rest shapes for failures and visible unresolved status. A prior shape is reused only with a compatible source/transform; otherwise use the current prepared rest shape. Atomic completeness and universal quality success are distinct.

Go: the result improves the scene and failure handling is usable. No-go: numerical clearance passes but vegetation looks melted, bundled, stretched or conspicuously quantized.

11. Reproducible validation matrix

11.1 Metrics and proposed tolerances

Let L be rest guide length, ℓj\ell_j rest segment length, r a root position and c the requested clearance.

  • Root drift: ∥r′−r∥\|r'-r\| and maximum drift of every pinned root-ring vertex.
  • Orientation drift: angle between intended and fitted root tangent/frame; report hard-pin and allowed-flexible cases separately.
  • Stretch: maximum |ℓ′j/ℓj−1||\ell'_j/\ell_j-1| and total arc-length error.
  • Clearance violation: max(0,c−dsurface)\max(0,c-d_{\mathrm{surface}}) under the declared physical thickness convention.
  • Penetration: signed overlap depth where solid classification is meaningful, plus surface-crossing counts; report separately from a missed positive clearance.
  • Shape: rest-relative curvature/twist change, maximum turn/curvature, mesh edge/area strain and within-blade self-intersections.
  • Repeatability: maximum geometric difference for repeated identical inputs with fixed backend/build/thread policy.
  • Output parity: maximum matching-vertex or surface discrepancy, normal/tangent differences, silhouette/depth discrepancy.
  • System behavior: phase times, peak/retained CPU/GPU memory, counters, live-generation count and cancellation latency.

For 0.2–0.5 m test blades, proposed starting gates are: root/output position tolerance from Section 6; hard root orientation ≤0.1°; P0 guide-length error ≤10−510^{-5} relative; P1/P2 guide-length error ≤0.5% with the same bound enforced in reach selection; zero detected forbidden crossings; clearance residual ≤max(0.02 mm,10−4L)\max(0.02\text{ mm},10^{-4}L); no degenerate/inverted/folded binding accepted.

Mesh strain and curvature limits are source-specific and must be fixed before comparing methods. A narrow ribbon’s longitudinal edge strain target of 5% is a proposed initial visual gate, not a physical material model. Report the full distribution, not only a maximum.

Tolerance floors must remain meaningfully below blade thickness/clearance. At unsupported scales or coordinates, report insufficient numerical resolution rather than widening tolerance until a test passes.

14. Direct answers

Is this practical for CyrusScatter?

Yes, for a deliberately supported static scope. Sparse contact fitting fits the supplied native placement architecture without replacing the scatter engine. Practicality is conditional on geometry delivery, asset preparation and the measured affected workload. No repository inspection or host experiment here proves a shipping implementation.

What is the simplest useful first version?

Root-fixed, prepared grass blades fitted around fixed scene objects or fixed obstacle-layer instances, with one stored pose and exact CPU mesh output for affected instances. Start with length-preserving procedural guide bending plus bounded static contact refinement, reliable segment/surface validation, explicit Update, and clear unresolved-contact feedback.

Does that version require actual physics?

No continuous physics simulation is required. It may use a numerical constrained fit. If XPBD or Jolt’s CPU rods materially improve the tested result, adopt the measured winner as the fitting backend without turning the product into a timeline simulation system.

Can ordinary imported grass models work, and what preparation is unavoidable?

Many can, if their geometry represents identifiable, sufficiently segmented blades. Roots, growth/rest direction, component identity and vertex-to-guide binding are unavoidable information. Automatic proposals can reduce authoring, but cards containing multiple painted blades, fused scans and opaque proxies require a narrower contract or explicit preparation.

How do we retain efficiency when each affected instance bends differently?

Keep N−A instances on the existing shared transform path; share rest geometry/binding; store only sparse guide states; reuse obstacle/source indices; and cache completed output. Exact unique rendered shapes still have a cost. Validate variants when K is small, and consider shaders/procedurals only if measured output bottlenecks justify their integration.

What should we measure before committing to an architecture?

Renderer path/parity first; then asset preparation, final-mesh contact and naturalness; then P0/P1/P2 total cost. Measure all phases, peak retained generations, cancellation and UI stalls, incremental changes, source/obstacle invalidation, Undo/Redo, save/reopen, and 100/1,000/10,000 affected-clump scenarios.

Which effects should be deferred, and why?

Wind, moving/deforming obstacles, animation replay and motion blur add temporal state and sampling contracts. Plant–plant/two-way interaction adds coupled islands and propagation. Broad-leaf folding, branching and arbitrary scans/proxies need richer asset/deformation models. Biological growth is a different phenomenon. A generic graph, mandatory GPU backend and whole-engine replacement are not justified by the initial static goal.
