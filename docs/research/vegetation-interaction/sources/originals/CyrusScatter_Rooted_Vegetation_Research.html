Engineering research and architecture evaluation

Rooted vegetation around obstacles

An engineering decision report for CyrusScatter: static fitting, asset contracts, contact, output and qualification.
24 October 2026 · Research snapshot

Recommendation: pursue a bounded static fitting prototype for prepared grass. Preserve roots, stable placements and source assets; fit only affected vegetation; validate the visible geometry; publish one complete reusable result.

Architecture choice: use compact source-bound guides and an engine-independent result. Compare simple geometric fitting with a small constraint/rod method. Prove Max 2027 viewport, final-render and Bake agreement before optimizing for scale.

Evidence boundary: read-only source inspection and primary-source research, with analytical cost estimates and proposed experiments. No repository modification, production implementation or Max runtime benchmark was performed.

Section 1

Executive recommendation

Conditional go for a small static feature; select the numerical method after contact and output tests.

Recommended decision

Proceed to a bounded Max 2027 research prototype of static, rooted vegetation fitting. Use prepared blade guides, all relevant fixed obstacles, sparse per-instance deformation, and one authoritative geometry evaluator. Start with a procedural arc-bend baseline and a focused constrained guide fit. Compare XPBD or Jolt CPU rods only through the same asset, contact and output contract. A general physics engine is optional.

The practical target is grass that stays planted while its shape bends around a stone. This is achievable in principle with a modest geometric representation, but three product risks remain unmeasured: whether ordinary production assets can be prepared economically; whether a bounded solver produces attractive shapes in competing contacts; and whether distinct bends can reach Nitrous, the selected final renderer and Bake without unacceptable geometry expansion. Confidence in the direction is medium. Confidence in any capacity or latency claim is low until measured.

Biggest unresolved risks

  • Renderer and memory: compact guides do not guarantee shared final geometry when every plant bends differently. A 12-instance parity experiment is the first gate, before a large solver benchmark.
  • Contact correctness: point-only checks miss segments that cross an obstacle with both endpoints outside. Clear guides also do not prove a wide visible blade is clear.
  • Asset preparation: root locations, base orientation, blade membership and sufficient segmentation are required information. Arbitrary scans and alpha cards do not reliably contain it.
  • Infeasibility and quality: pinned roots inside solids, closed gaps and opposing contacts may have no acceptable solution. A bounded attempt must report that outcome while preserving the old publication.
  • Lifecycle: renderer readers, old/new display resources, Undo and saved bindings can retain more memory than the numerical solve. Existing synchronous workers do not establish cancellable asynchronous lifetimes. [S06]

Section 2

Feature definition and initial limits

A static shape operation attached to accepted placements, with explicit support and failure boundaries.

Proposed feature name: Fit vegetation around obstacles. For accepted placements of supported vegetation, capture the selected ordinary scene obstacles and/or a selected Scatter obstacle layer, find every plant whose reachable geometry can interact, and compute an alternative shape with its root position and base orientation anchored. Store that completed result independently of navigation and timeline evaluation. The existing source model, placement transform and surviving instance identity remain authoritative.

Smallest coherent contract

  1. Support a segmented blade and a disconnected clump of identifiable segmented blades. Require confirmed root/base frames, rest guides and deterministic vertex bindings. Positive uniform source/instance scale and rigid transforms are the first qualification subset.
  2. Support a sphere/box for controlled tests and evaluated triangle obstacles for the useful scene case. Closed solids are the default solid-contact contract; thin/open surfaces require an explicit two-sided sheet mode or a closed artist proxy.
  3. Use a bounded one-way solve: vegetation responds to fixed obstacle snapshots. The stone and obstacle-layer instances do not move in response. No vegetation self-contact or vegetation-to-vegetation interaction is included.
  4. Fit after ordinary placement, Edit, projection, spacing, cleanup and refill have produced accepted rows. The contact stage changes shape only. A new population edit reruns the relevant preceding stages and then fits their accepted successor.
  5. Publish one coherent placement-plus-deformation generation. A failed or cancelled successor leaves the previous complete result readable. First-time failure leaves the previously visible undeformed scatter and reports that no fit was published.
  6. Use explicit Update/Fit for the first prototype. The shipping behavior must honor the enclosing Manual/Live contract: Manual preserves pending edits; Live coalesces only relevant changes. A prototype lacking Live qualification must expose that limit explicitly.

A layer may contain supported and unsupported sources, but inclusion is an authored choice made before fitting. Do not silently skip failed plants and label the layer collision-correct. The first release should reject a failed requested successor as a whole; an optional “keep verified subset” mode would require a separately designed mixed-status publication and is deferred.

Static ownership now, animation later

Persist the completed rest-relative guide state, source-binding identity and source snapshot needed to reconstruct its geometry. There is no requirement to save velocities or a physics world. Store numerical method and schema versions for interpretation, without making the solver’s private binary format the scene format. This leaves room for another solver while keeping the same publication and output contract.

Section 3

Comparison of viable approaches

Representation, numerical method, time integration and output transport are separate decisions.

A rod is a mechanical representation; XPBD is a constraint-solving method. A rod can be solved statically, and a static guide can be fitted without integrating motion. Likewise, guide/skinning data is a mesh-binding choice rather than a promise of renderer instancing. Comparing those choices separately prevents an engine name from deciding the whole product.

A. What a procedural baseline should test

For normalized root-to-tip coordinate u, a root-weighted displacement can use w(u)=u²(3−2u), so w(0)=0 and its derivative is zero at the base. An obstacle direction field then supplies displacement w(u)d(u). This is a proposed visual baseline, not an elastic law. Direct displacement generally changes length. Prefer a piecewise arc/rotation construction with fixed segment lengths, followed by contact validation, when only gentle one-sided bends are required.

A closest-point normal can guide a blade away from one stone, but nearest features switch at corners; normals from opposite obstacles can cancel if averaged. A field that pushes every point outward can flatten a clump, introduce kinks, or direct it through a second obstacle. Repeated project-smooth-length-repair cycles can undo each other. Limit passes, track length/contact residuals after the final pass, and report failure instead of treating repeated projection as a proof of convergence.

B. The smallest useful reduced model

Use one oriented polyline per visible blade for the reference asset. For dense clumps, test a small number of meaningful blade groups only after that reference works. A representative guide may deform several nearby, similarly oriented blades, but their actual roots must remain fixed and the binding must preserve individual rest offsets. The smallest useful G is an empirical asset-quality decision, not a constant for all grass.

A broad cage is attractive for artist shaping but may rotate the entire tuft and squash the base. Guide frames provide a more direct root-to-tip coordinate and a measurable stretch contract. SideFX’s Guide Deform documentation separates precomputed guide capture from deformation and now includes shape-preserving quasi-static correction. This is useful workflow evidence for shape-first plus correction, not evidence of Cyrus collision correctness or speed. [S16]

C. A proposed static objective and the solver comparison

Static fitting problem - proposed, not implemented

Choose guide coordinates/frames q to minimize:
E(q) = w_b E_rest_bend(q) + w_s E_shape(q) + w_t E_twist(q).
Subject to fixed root position and base frame; bounded segment stretch;
signed_clearance(segment j, solid m) ≥ radius j + clearance;
and an authored maximum bend. All relevant m participate.

Here signed_clearance is the minimum positive-outside signed distance along the entire segment; a BVH-only path must enforce surface separation plus outside classification and no crossing. For an open sheet, use the separately declared thickness/side contract. E_rest_bend measures deviation from the source’s rest curvature rather than from a straight line. E_shape can weakly attract points toward their initial positions so a contact does not flatten the entire tuft. A frame/angle parameterization can preserve segment lengths by construction; a positional method instead needs an explicit length residual. Hard roots and stretch bounds can make the problem infeasible. Nonconvex obstacles and rest-energy minima mean a local solve may fail even when another feasible shape exists.

Compare three bounded numerical contenders with identical input guides, collision oracle and mesh decoder: (1) root-preserving procedural arcs, (2) a focused static constrained guide fit, and (3) an XPBD/rod relaxation comparator. The second may use projected constraints or a small nonlinear optimizer; its complexity must remain proportionate to the narrow contract. Test Jolt CPU rods as the engine-backed form of the third if build/contact evidence warrants it. Choose by time and memory to acceptable visible geometry, including preparation and output.

XPBD accumulates constraint multipliers and uses scaled compliance. Its scalar update can be written Δλ = (−C(q)−α̃λ)/(Σ w_i‖∇C_i‖²+α̃), with α̃=α/Δt². For unilateral contact the multiplier/sign convention must enforce non-attraction. The paper improves the connection between compliance and elastic response; its limitations still identify artificial compliance from finite iterations. A low-budget result is therefore not independent of iteration count, timestep or substep choices. Static use must fix and record these conventions and compare final residuals. [S11]

DER explicitly represents a centerline and a material frame, including rest curvature and twist; the original treatment uses a quasistatic material frame with dynamic centerline motion. Cosserat formulations add oriented segments with stretch/shear and bend/twist relationships. Both are relevant to long stems and anisotropic blades; neither supplies imported-mesh roots or an automatic obstacle-contact guarantee. A fixed root point alone permits the base tangent or twist to change: the first segment or base frame needs its own clamp. [S12] [S13]

Mass-spring chains are easy to assemble, but spring stiffness, damping and timestep can create difficult tuning or stretching. Implicit or local-global spring methods can address weaknesses of a naive explicit chain, but bring their own system/iteration cost and do not supply the missing asset/contact contract. [S64] A chain also lacks a material frame for leaf width/twist unless one is added. Dynamic settling with gravity and damping finds a history-dependent rest state; it is not automatically the best static fit to an artist’s preferred shape. Defaulting gravity to a world axis would be especially inappropriate when the imported rest mesh already expresses sag.

D-E. When a different model earns its cost

A blade whose width and torsion matter may need a ribbon with transverse structure, or a centerline with a stable cross-section frame. Broad leaves that fold along multiple directions can justify a shell. Treat a quad strip as a sheet with geometric thickness/contact offset, not a closed solid; volumetric tetrahedra add little value for thin grass. A photogrammetry clump should not be tetrahedralized merely because an engine exposes soft bodies.

Variants optimize a validated reference fit. Exact matching states can share one mesh; approximate clusters require contact validation for every assigned instance. A visually similar variant may penetrate a narrow gap. Cap K, key the cache by source/binding/method revisions, and fall back to exact geometry when error exceeds budget. Variants establish no capacity guarantee.

Proposed source binding and decoder

A shared SourceBinding owns a source identity, evaluated rest-geometry hash, topology/map-channel signatures, root/base frames, rest guide points/frames, segment lengths and thickness envelopes, vertex-to-guide assignments, and a binding-schema revision. It also owns the numeric source snapshot required to keep a published result stable when the live source changes. It contains no borrowed mutable Max mesh pointer.

Guide-to-mesh reconstruction - proposed

For a vertex assigned to guide g at arc coordinate s:
p′ = c_g(s) + R_g(s) R_rest,g(s)^T [p_rest − c_rest,g(s)].
For a multi-guide binding, blend compatible transforms with fixed weights;
then validate roots, visible surface clearance and local distortion.

All terms in the decoder use one orthonormal-frame coordinate convention: transform rest centers, offsets and solved guides into the same local solve frame, including uniform scale, then apply the recorded solve-to-world transform once. Store the segment index and interpolation coordinate, plus a rest-frame offset and any necessary influence weights. Interpolate frame rotations with sign-consistent normalized quaternion interpolation or a documented parallel-transport rule; avoid linearly blending arbitrary matrices and assuming rigidity. Hard root vertices use an anchored mapping. Exact guide lengths do not guarantee inextensible visible ribbons: offset edges and blended regions can stretch or shear, so measure decoded-mesh distortion separately. The first decoder should favor one guide per blade, which is easier to inspect than a broad blend. More general skinning can introduce shrinkage, candy-wrapper artifacts or foldovers; none is cured simply by reducing the numerical guide count.

Guide points alone do not define orientation about the tangent. Parallel transport supplies a smooth minimal-twist frame for simple blades; authored rest twist must be retained. A collapsed segment or a near-180-degree tangent reversal needs a deterministic fallback or rejection. SideFX Point Deform warns that wire-like deformers can produce unstable local transforms; its Guide Deform page explicitly supports orientation attributes for curve deformers. That supports testing framed bindings instead of assuming generic nearest-point deformation is sufficient. [S17] [S16]

Visible mesh quality, channels and segmentation

Keep face topology, material IDs, map-channel values/indices and smoothing-group intent when the source topology is unchanged. UV seams remain separate even when geometric positions coincide. Normals require a deliberate policy: rebuild from the deformed surface while honoring smoothing/explicit-normal rules, or transport explicit normals using a valid local deformation differential and renormalize. Tangents must be regenerated or transported consistently with the chosen UV/normal convention, including handedness under mirroring. Simply copying source normals creates incorrect lighting. Max provides normal/cache operations, but they do not decide this policy automatically. [S29]

Adding guide segments cannot bend an unsegmented render card smoothly: only its existing vertices move. As a geometric preparation criterion, for local curvature κ and target chord error ε, the circular-arc estimate ε≈κℓ²/8 gives ℓ≤√(8ε/κ) for render-edge length ℓ. This small-angle estimate is a design aid, not a universal segmentation count. Start with 4/8/16 guide segments and compatible render tessellation; inspect close silhouettes and narrow-gap contact. Preserve the original source and make any subdivision a distinct preparation copy.

For alpha cards, full rectangular collision is conservative relative to the opaque silhouette but can over-clear a stone. Sampling the opacity texture into a collision outline introduces filtering, threshold, UV and animation dependencies. Defer that to a separate asset feature; v1 should either use an explicit geometric proxy or label card-level collision. A picture containing several grass blades cannot acquire independent roots by bending its four corners.

Broad phase: select geometry that can reach the obstacle

Build or reuse an obstacle broad phase over bounds and source triangle structures. Query each relevant plant using its full rest bounds expanded by allowed deformation reach, blade thickness and clearance. A root or object-center radius alone misses a long overhanging blade. If the current deformation can reach outside the initial bound, use a conservative maximum reach based on total guide length, or expand and requery before accepting a move. An artist influence radius must not silently disable contact along a blade that is inside the declared supported solve.

Include every obstacle whose bounds overlap the reachable region. A nearby second stone, thin slab or overhang may be more important than the nearest object center. A grid is simple for comparable sizes but suffers with large size variation or clustered cells; a BVH handles heterogeneous bounds and supports triangle culling. Reuse an existing spatial utility only after confirming that its contract supports extended segments and the required metric, rather than assuming the placement-clearance grid is already a surface-contact engine. For obstacles from another Scatter layer, retain one triangle BVH per distinct evaluated source and a top-level structure over accepted instance transforms/bounds. Narrow phase: segments, surfaces and a final visible-geometry check

Use segment/capsule-to-triangle distance and intersection queries for a finite-thickness guide. A segment that enters and exits a closed stone can have both endpoints outside, so testing only guide vertices is insufficient. A point sample is not a substitute unless an independently derived conservative sampling bound is enforced. Generate contacts for all relevant obstacles; stable feature IDs and warm-started normals may reduce switching but must not conceal a missing contact.

For wide blades, a capsule around a centerline is either too small to enclose the mesh or so large that it over-clears corners. Measure a per-segment binding envelope and use it conservatively, then validate the decoded visible triangles against obstacle triangles and clearance. Surface crossings require edge/face or triangle-pair tests, not only vertex signs. Also classify containment: an entire blade component inside a solid can have no surface crossing yet still be invalid. If the envelope causes unacceptable gaps, a ribbon-aware narrow phase or richer source profile is needed. This is a concrete way to falsify the “few guides are sufficient” assumption.

A watertight, consistently oriented manifold without self-intersections supports an ordinary solid inside/outside contract; other inputs require a separately declared solid-classification rule. A nearest unsigned distance does not tell which side is outside. Winding-based or pseudo-normal signing can improve numerical behavior, but an open or nonmanifold surface still needs an explicit semantic policy. OpenVDB’s signed mesh conversion requires a closed surface, although it documents tolerance of some degeneracy/self-intersection; its unsigned conversion is a separate operation. Do not turn that implementation robustness into a promise that arbitrary open rocks define a volume. [S19] [S21]

For a deliberate thin/open obstacle, use a two-sided sheet with declared thickness and a stable side chosen from the rest configuration or authored orientation. If a blade starts exactly on the sheet, normal direction can be ambiguous; require a side/proxy decision instead of random ejection. Concavities and narrow gaps need multiple active contacts. Averaging opposing normals or using a convex hull that fills a cavity changes the feature’s meaning. A chosen convex proxy is an artist approximation and must be visible in diagnostics.

Define a total geometric error budget: query approximation + obstacle-proxy error + guide-envelope/binding error + variant error + output-evaluation error. Choose clearance margins accordingly and validate the final geometry with an independent higher-quality oracle. An exact signed distance is 1-Lipschitz, but sampled, truncated or incorrectly signed fields may not retain an assumed bound; do not claim a universal √3h safety margin for every SDF implementation.

Scale, initial penetration and local coupling

Store physical distances with a declared conversion boundary, and solve in a nearby origin frame to reduce large-coordinate loss. Use double precision for host snapshot transforms, distance predicates and residual accumulation where justified; packed float guide/render data is separately qualified. Reject nonfinite values, degenerate triangles and extreme aspect ratios under a documented policy. A tolerance must be stated in physical units and relative to plant dimensions, not as one unexplained scene-unit epsilon.

Initial interpenetration needs a recovery stage. Movable blade sections may be projected or initialized along a feasible side, followed by the full constrained fit and validation. A pinned base inside the solid is a structural failure. Methods such as IPC demonstrate strong contact guarantees under specific assumptions, including an initially intersection-free state and continuous collision-aware steps; they are useful references for validation rigor, not a cheap general recovery solution for this task. [S22]

With fixed obstacles and no inter-plant constraints, plants can be fitted independently. Guides within one blade group may still couple through their shared binding or shape term. Vegetation-to-vegetation contacts connect neighboring plants into islands that can extend beyond the selected region; simply freezing the boundary changes the problem. That extension must be evaluated separately and is deferred.

Other Scatter layers and pipeline ordering

An obstacle layer supplies a committed placement/source snapshot. Grass A may depend on stone layer B, but A cannot make B move during its fit. Record B’s owner, source lineage, stable instance IDs and committed generation. A pending Manual B edit is not the same as a new committed obstacle: A’s status should name the generation it uses. An explicit update transaction can stage both owners in dependency order, but ordinary reads must not secretly update B.

Reject A→B→A interaction cycles in the first version. An explicit frozen external snapshot is a possible later escape hatch, but must be labeled as such and not silently use whichever generation happens to exist. Cycle detection on a small list of layer bindings does not require a generic graph framework. For acyclic dependencies, invalidation and publication order are explicit.

The contact stage follows accepted placements, including protected Edit rows and cleanup/refill. Later ordinary Relax, projection or acceptance must not move roots or delete plants from the completed fit. If a relevant earlier setting changes, calculate a new base successor and then fit it; preserve the previous whole result until both stages pass. Shape contact is not another acceptance/rejection pass. The existing pipeline and preservation rules support this ordering. [S05] [S06] [S07]

Atomic publication, history and persistence

Admit the union of unique live resources before the update: old publication, new publication, snapshots, scratch, output staging and history. Build the successor privately. Once every required canonical output is valid and retained display staging succeeds, recheck the complete relevant-input token and promote on the host thread. A late cancel, stale token, allocation failure or device-stage failure discards the candidate and retains the old complete state. Within an explicitly combined multi-layer update, separate per-layer commits are unsafe if they expose mismatched promised generations. In ordinary Manual/Live operation, B may publish while A intentionally retains its complete older fit and becomes Pending/Stale against B; that is different from mixing incompatible base/deformation data inside A.

Renderer-owned translation and acceleration allocations may occur later and cannot all be prestaged by Scatter. A later renderer allocation failure should fail that render visibly without corrupting the published numerical result. Do not claim that atomic Scatter publication guarantees every future renderer allocation will succeed. Immutable application geometry also does not make mutable SDK Mesh adapters safe for concurrent cache mutation; Section 6 specifies their separate lifetime.

Use one bounded authored Update/Fit transaction and retain previous completed data by shared generation references. Do not add a full geometry copy to Undo for every UI tick. Live coalescing, scalar-setting Undo and explicit fit Undo need a defined policy: restoring authored state may legitimately leave Manual pending output, while undoing a committed Fit restores the previous completed generation. A job carries the authored/Undo revision that authorized it, so Undo during work makes a stale completion ineligible. Never clear the host Undo stack to meet memory limits.

Save enough to show/render the last completed fit without running a solve on reopen: a versioned source binding and owned rest-geometry snapshot (deduplicated per source), stable placement correspondence, compact guide state/frames, units, decoder version, output-critical channels and input receipts. Material/texture references may remain external under the host’s normal rules; missing textures cannot be promised identical shading. A settings-only export is a distinct explicit format, not an implicit omission of completed state.

On reopen with missing obstacles, preserve the saved completed geometry and flag that Update is unavailable until bindings are repaired. A missing live source can likewise preserve the embedded owned snapshot, with an explicit stale/missing-source status. If a scene only contains external references and no rest snapshot, exact reconstruction cannot be promised. Invalid topology/hash/schema must not be rebound by vertex order or name. Current schema 59 and existing Edit-copy limitations mean save/copy/merge identity remapping needs a new reviewed version boundary and specific tests, not an assumed automatic extension. [S06] [S07]

Mutable SDK mesh lifetime and callback threads

Canonical numerical arrays may be immutable. The SDK Mesh returned through a raw pointer is mutable and may carry renderer-owned render data. Pin the relevant application generation and its adapter for the renderer’s actual use period; holding a shared reference only inside the callback is insufficient. If a renderer mutates caches, use qualified adapter copies or serialized ownership rather than sharing a supposedly immutable Mesh across unrelated readers. needDelete controls deletion responsibility, not all concurrency hazards. [S24] [S29]

Max’s interactive-render session documentation explicitly places update work on a dedicated thread and warns against scene access there. This does not identify the thread of every object callback; it does invalidate a blanket “rendering is always main-thread work” assumption. Pre-evaluate and prepare output on the host thread. Callbacks must consume completed owned data without invoking Max scene evaluation, solving or unsafe synchronous marshalling. Trace caller threads, update/release order and destruction for each selected renderer. [S28]

Test repeated render, interactive restart, cancellation, owner deletion, Undo during rendering and plugin shutdown. Retirement must wait for readers and GPU fences under the corresponding API rules. Renderer acceleration structures and private mesh copies are additional measured memory, not hidden inside the guide payload estimate.

Bake as an output boundary

Bake materializes exactly the completed accepted deformation, preserving original source nodes. Use bounded host publication batches with one declared Undoable operation and an output admission check. Bounded paging limits orchestration memory but cannot make the final baked geometry free. Shared exact variants can become instances if the chosen Bake mode explicitly supports that; unique bends require distinct geometry. A proxy-export option must first materialize compatible geometry or use a verified renderer-specific exporter. No fitting or implicit source preparation should happen merely because the artist invokes render or Bake.

Jolt: relevant rods, incomplete product contract

Jolt’s rod API has length, rest Bishop frame, compliance and a static-rod inverse-mass option. Its own documentation warns that an unconstrained rod orientation can spin about its axis. Therefore root position and base orientation need distinct tests; pinning one particle is not enough to establish an anchored blade frame. Current CPU rod samples include plant-like structures, but sample counts are not performance evidence. [S40] [S60]

In v5.6.0, the inspected CPU soft-body implementation determines collision planes through CollideSoftBodyVertices, then handles rod constraints separately. This is direct evidence of a vertex-based contact path, not proof of segment/capsule contact. The decisive experiment is a long segment whose endpoints are outside a stone while its middle intersects. More subdivisions may hide that case at some scales without proving correctness. Reinspect the actual 5.6.1 pin if it is selected, and compare with the independent segment/surface validator. [S41]

Jolt jobs and barriers require safe completion; the inspected JobSystem interface does not establish arbitrary immediate cancellation. Stop future bounded steps, drain outstanding jobs, then discard the unpublished result. Do not destroy a live world from another thread to cancel it. Repeatability is conditional on build, inputs and ordering; broad/narrow-phase ordering and floating-point choices must be controlled and tested. No cross-platform bitwise guarantee is proposed for Scatter. [S43] [S39]

The v5.6.0 build supports C++17 and configurable CRT, exceptions/RTTI, CPU instruction features and compute backends. A CPU-only experiment should disable unneeded GPU/compute and sample paths and match the host plugin’s ABI/runtime settings. Do not inherit upstream static-CRT or instruction defaults blindly. Minor engine versions can change API and binary serialization; persist Scatter-owned guide data, not opaque Jolt state expected to survive upgrades. [S42] [S45]

PBD and PhysX: dependency boundaries

PBD’s current source declares 2.2.2 while its latest tagged GitHub release remains 2.2.0 from December 2022. The selected development build depends on Eigen, pinned Discregrid and GenericParameters; optional/default OpenMP and compiler settings need isolation from the Max project. The simulation layer uses a process-static TimeManager, so independent concurrent jobs require a reentrancy review or lower-level kernels. A direct rod solver’s acyclic internal structure does not make external obstacle contacts a globally acyclic problem. [S46] [S47] [S48] [S49] [S61]

PhysX 5.12’s deformable surface/volume APIs imply GPU buffers, synchronization and CPU readback for ordinary host/Bake output. Its Windows documentation names Visual Studio 2019/2022 x64 and CUDA-compatible hardware/driver conditions; CPU-only build exceptions do not apply to GPU-only deformable actors. The simulate/fetchResults lifecycle also requires completion before scene mutation. Adding this stack would increase hardware and distribution scope before its model has earned a place in slender-grass fitting. [S55] [S56] [S58] [S59]

The official Max 2027 SDK Requirements link returned 404 during this study. The exact required toolset must therefore be confirmed from the installed Max 2027 SDK documentation/sample settings before any build. Match compiler, architecture, Windows SDK, CRT and exception/RTTI assumptions, then compile, link and load in that host. Do not infer the host compiler from a renderer redistributable, and do not imply Max 2026 compatibility from a successful 2027 build.

Artist workflow and controls

A small fitting workflow with explicit pending, completed and failed states.

  1. Enable Fit vegetation on the intended layer and choose the supported source profiles. Source preparation displays roots, growth direction, rest guides and the collision envelope before the source is marked Ready.
  2. Pick ordinary obstacle objects or a committed obstacle layer. Each binding shows whether it uses evaluated triangles, a closed proxy or an explicitly supported sheet. Highlighting must show the collision representation that will actually be used.
  3. Set clearance and the few shape controls, then select Preview guides/affected plants if useful. In Manual mode this changes authored settings only and shows Pending; the existing plants remain from the last completed Update.

  5. Use Reset deformation as an explicit shape operation. It removes the chosen stored deformation entries while preserving base placements and identities. Any source-preparation change remains a separate authored action.

Expected edits

Moving a stone leaves the previous shape visible in Manual, then refits both newly affected plants and those freed at the old location on Update. Live may debounce drag changes and publish only a complete matching successor; it should not show a sequence of partially replaced clumps. Removing the stone lets previously bent plants return toward their source rest shape. Source edits require binding validation. Density edits can change which placements exist under existing Scatter semantics, but a bend-only edit must preserve the base ID/transform set.

An Update failure message should be concrete: “Fit not published: 7 roots are inside Rock_03; last completed result remains visible.” Provide Select affected / Show contacts / Open source preparation, not an automatic fix that slides roots. If the finite solver merely found no acceptable shape, say that; do not present an uncertified local failure as proof that no possible geometry exists.

A Manual scene with pending obstacle edits intentionally displays a result fitted to the old obstacle snapshot, while the live scene obstacle may have moved. Show that mismatch clearly. Geometry-only preview of current obstacles can be useful, but must not be labeled as current fitted contact. This is consistent with existing published-versus-pending Scatter semantics. [S06]

The later sweeps increase one bound at a time only after measured admission proves the requested combination fits.

Use an explicit maximum contact count and allocation-failure injection. The exact byte and latency thresholds are product decisions to set on the chosen reference workstation before P3: B_CPU, B_VRAM, B_update and B_cancel. A gate is pass only when measured peak≤budget and accepted-quality p95 update/cancel latency≤the corresponding budget. This report cannot supply a credible millisecond promise without a target machine, renderer and asset set.

How the numerical comparison can reject the attractive option

  • If procedural arcs pass the required contact and visual set, prefer them over a richer solver. Keep failed complex cases explicitly unsupported rather than silently claiming general fitting.
  • If arcs fail but a few static constraints meet the same gates, choose that focused fit. Require residual checks after the last length/shape correction, not merely before it.
  • If Jolt at the selected pin meets the segment/visible-mesh contact test and reduces implementation burden, it can win. If custom segment contact, custom root clamps and extensive state handling erase the reuse advantage, reject it for v1.
  • If no compact guide model produces acceptable broad-blade geometry, either narrow assets to slender blades or justify a ribbon/shell profile. Do not hide the problem with more guide iterations.
  • If unique geometry expansion dominates despite cheap guides, investigate validated variants or a targeted renderer evaluator. Defer scale claims until the complete output path is measured.

P0 and P1 can be developed independently once their shared source-binding/decoder contract is fixed. Their evidence must meet before a large engine integration. The final report from each phase should include exact source/binary identities, fixture files, input receipts, numeric and visual outcomes, observed limits and the decision taken.

Direct answers to the seven decisions

QuestionAnswer
Is this practical for CyrusScatter?Yes, conditionally, for static one-way fitting of prepared slender vegetation in Max 2027. The source architecture has suitable stable native placement ownership, but renderer parity, contact quality and full peak memory must pass the bounded gates. No production readiness or capacity has been demonstrated.
What is the simplest useful first version?Prepared segmented blades/disconnected clumps with confirmed roots and frames; fixed ordinary objects or a committed obstacle layer; root-preserving procedural arcs or a focused static guide fit; sparse completed shapes; explicit Update; accurate CPU-reference viewport/render/Bake output.
Does it require actual physics?No. A static geometric constrained fit can deliver the intended image. Use physical rods/XPBD/Jolt only if they outperform simpler fitting on the same contact, shape and integration tests. Force-calibrated mechanics can also be static and would need a separate validation contract.
Can imported grass work, and what preparation is unavoidable?Suitable imported segmented blades can work. Roots/base orientation, blade/guide correspondence, rest shape, vertex binding, sufficient render segmentation and a thickness policy are unavoidable information. Automatic proposals require confirmation; arbitrary scans, multi-blade alpha images and inaccessible proxies are not automatic plant rigs.
How can different bends remain efficient?Share immutable source geometry/bindings and obstacle acceleration, fit only affected IDs, retain compact guides and reuse completed static output. Expect unique final geometry to cost O(AV) unless exact variants or a qualified evaluator preserve sharing; measure those consumers before promising scale.
What must be measured before committing?End-to-end output parity and lifetime; asset preparation/quality; segment and visible-mesh contact; root/frame/strain residuals; total time to accepted shape; peak old/new/scratch/GPU/renderer/history memory; cancellation, dependencies, Undo and save/reopen; mostly-unaffected reuse counters.
What should be deferred, and why?Wind/continuous simulation, moving roots, biological growth, vegetation self/contact coupling, two-way motion, arbitrary proxy deformation, broad-leaf shells and a generic GPU/graph system.
