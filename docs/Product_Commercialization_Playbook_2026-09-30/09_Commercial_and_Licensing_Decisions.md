# Stage 09 — Commercial and licensing decisions

**Updated 2026-10-02.** We will build our own licensing service. This stage uses the current [decision register](../licensing/DECISIONS.md); it no longer maintains a second table of proposed prices, durations or permissions.

## Goal

Choose a small offer that we can implement, explain and support. Separate the selected own-service direction from unapproved customer terms. Individual and assigned studio seats are the recommended initial scope; floating and other enterprise options have their own gates.

## Your work and the owner's decisions

1. Review D01–D08 and the open B01–B12 choices in the decision register.
2. Supply concrete artist/studio needs from authorized feedback.
3. Write a customer example for each choice: two Max processes, laptop/workstation, a studio adding five seats, an outage, expired maintenance and a lost machine.
4. Record an approved choice or explicit deferral with owner/date, scope and tests. Blank fields are not approval.
5. Use the same register for the operation/continuity matrix. Resolve unlicensed local rendering and any perpetual-offline promise before customer enforcement.
6. Carry approved terms into the offer/pricing stage; keep catalogue pricing outside the native client.

Engineering can begin the isolated L0/L1 experiments using clearly named test policies while terms are open. It must not turn historical example durations into default customer contracts.

## Engineering work

Use the [roadmap](../licensing/ROADMAP.md) to report feasibility, implementation cost, tests and remaining limits. In particular, distinguish assigned people from devices and floating reservations, and show why disconnected permissions affect transfer/revocation timing.

Revisit cost-sensitive choices with owned-service hosting, authentication, signing, support and recovery evidence. Commercial licensing-platform selection is no longer a required step.

## Completion and status

The document consolidation is complete. Business terms, operation-policy approval and licensing runtime tests remain OPEN/NOT TESTED. Stage completion requires the applicable B-rows to be approved or explicitly deferred, with customer examples and acceptance tests.

Do not publish trial, offline, perpetual, render-worker or SLA guarantees before the corresponding decision and qualification. This statement limits product promises; it does not block a configurable internal prototype.
