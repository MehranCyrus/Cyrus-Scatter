# Stage 14 — Landing page content plan

## Goal

Prepare the website's section-by-section content brief, supported claims and media requirements. Do not build or publish the website in this stage.

## Why this matters

An artist should quickly understand the task Cyrus solves, see proof, check compatibility and understand the offer. Unsupported speed, compatibility or licensing promises would create avoidable support and trust problems.

## What we currently know

- **PROPOSED:** lead workflows and positioning in Stage 05 need manual/beta validation.
- **VERIFIED IN SOURCE:** features in Stage 03 exist to differing degrees; source existence alone does not approve public usability claims.
- **REQUIRES MANUAL 3DS MAX TEST:** the Stage 07 matrix controls host/renderer/workflow claims.
- **UNKNOWN:** final price, trial, offline terms and public availability. These remain owner decisions.
- [Landing page claims matrix](Landing_Page_Claims_Matrix.md) is the approval register; every feature/support claim must have an ID and proof.

## My tasks

- [ ] Choose one audience and lead task.
- [ ] Fill the content brief using actual Stage 13 assets.
- [ ] Write concise copy that matches qualified features.
- [ ] Link claims to evidence and approved support rows.
- [ ] Prepare FAQ answers from real beta questions.
- [ ] Review CTA/offer wording after Stage 15 decisions.

## Engineering / Codex tasks

- Validate technical copy, counts, limits, compatibility and download identity.
- Supply approved install/recovery/support documentation.
- Flag claims invalidated by source, renderer or package changes.
- Later website implementation is a separate assignment.

## Boss / Product-owner decisions

Approve positioning, brand, public availability, commercial terms, CTA and final claims. A draft page can exist before pricing; purchase/trial wording cannot be approved before its operational and commercial gates.

## Step-by-step procedure

1. Write a one-sentence value proposition for the qualified lead workflow.
2. Draft the sections below; leave unsupported content explicitly pending.
3. Attach approved asset IDs from Stage 13.
4. Attach claim IDs and exact build/support proof.
5. Review the page as a new user: what it does, what works on my machine, how to learn, how to buy/test, how to recover.
6. After Stage 15, replace pricing/trial placeholders with approved terms.
7. Have engineering and the owner review the final brief; publication remains a later Stage 16 decision.

### FAQ worksheet

Answer only after the referenced decisions/tests:

1. Which exact Max updates and renderer builds are qualified?
2. Why is displayed population different from final population?
3. What happens to CS Edit when seed, source or layer identity changes?
4. What inputs does Surface Analyzer accept?
5. Can I render a saved scene on another workstation or worker?
6. What happens when the trial, maintenance or offline entitlement expires?
7. How do I transfer/recover a machine activation?
8. How do I uninstall, recover a scene and roll back?
9. Which assets may be shared with a downloadable demo scene?
10. How do I report a reproducible problem?

### Presentation checks for the future website

Use readable text, labelled screenshots, captions/transcripts, restrained animation and clear CTAs. Keep support limitations close to relevant claims. Plan accessible navigation, mobile layouts, compressed media/posters and accurate page descriptions when the website is implemented. No analytics, forms or external services are installed by this content plan.

## Evidence to collect

Section copy cards, claim approvals, asset register, support matrix, tested FAQ links, approved offer and owner review. Link each public statement to its build/date; remove or qualify it when evidence expires.
