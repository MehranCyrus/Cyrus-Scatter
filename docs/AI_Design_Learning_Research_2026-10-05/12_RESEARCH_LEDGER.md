# Primary-source research ledger

Access date and cutoff: **2026-10-05**. This is a targeted engineering literature review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a claim to have reproduced these methods. Search covered procedural scene/vegetation synthesis, interactive evolution, preference learning and personalization, active search, visual/spatial interpretation, agent evaluation, graphs, MCP and host integration.

Only primary papers, author project pages, official repositories and official documentation support recommendations. Search engines and secondary summaries were used for discovery, not as validation. Paper dates below distinguish original submissions from later revisions/venues where relevant. Current repository/documentation pages can change; pin an exact revision when adopting code or a model.

Reading depth is deliberately explicit: **abstract** means claims were screened from the primary abstract/metadata; **selected sections** means relevant methods/results were inspected, not necessarily every appendix; **documentation** means the relevant official contract/README sections were read. No paper benchmark was rerun and no third-party model was installed.

## Human preferences, search and optimization

| ID / primary source | Date/status and reading depth | Useful lesson and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S07 — [Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03741) | 2017; primary abstract/overview. | Pairwise human judgments can train a reward used by an interactive policy. This supports the general feedback idea; it does not make full RL necessary for parameter search. |
| S08 — [Deep Interactive Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08230) | 2018; primary abstract. | Human selection can guide a generative search space. Cyrus can test local recipe mutation around favourites without adopting the paper's generative representation. |
| S09 — [Multi-attribute Bayesian optimization with interactive preference learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/astudillo20a.html) | AISTATS 2020; primary paper abstract. | Expensive design search can use uncertainty about utility and comparisons. Useful low-dimensional baseline; mixed recipe structure and Max costs require a separate experiment. |
| S10 — [BoTorch BOPE tutorial](https://botorch.org/docs/v0.17.0/tutorials/bope) | Versioned official tutorial, accessed 2026-10-05; model/acquisition example inspected. | Shows preference exploration over outcomes with pairwise utility modelling. A tutorial is a starting implementation reference, not a guarantee of better landscape search. |
| S11 — [Illuminating search spaces by mapping elites](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909) | 2015; primary abstract. | Keep a repertoire across meaningful descriptors rather than only the top scalar score. Cyrus must validate the descriptors and artist value of that diversity. |
| S12 — [ImageReward](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/33646ef0ed554145eab65f6250fab0c9-Abstract-Conference.html) | NeurIPS 2023; primary abstract. | Image preference models can learn from human comparisons. General text-to-image preferences should be tested as a baseline, not treated as professional planting authority. |
| S13 — [Personalizing RLHF with Variational Preference Learning](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/5e1c255653eb98cef13f45b2d337c882-Abstract-Conference.html) | NeurIPS 2024; primary abstract. | Heterogeneous preferences motivate conditional rather than universal scoring. Does not determine our studio's decision policy or prove visual transfer. |
| S14 — [Capturing Individual Human Preferences with Reward Features](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/761bc7f095f927e5a5de31ed4154bb45-Abstract-Conference.html) | NeurIPS 2025; primary abstract. | Shared reward features with individual adaptation are worth comparing to separate profiles. Domain, data scale and evaluation differ from Cyrus. |
| S15 — [PreferThinker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00609) | Original 1 Nov 2025; v4 26 Jun 2026; authors report ICLR 2026 acceptance. Abstract/version history. | Predict-then-assess preference profiles suggest editable profile hypotheses and dimension-specific assessments. Reasoning-trained scoring is not automatically better than a small ranker on our data. |
| S16 — [PrefGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06020) | 4 Dec 2025 preprint; primary abstract. | Multimodal preference representations can condition image generation. Transfer to a procedural recipe generator is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated capability. |
| S17 — [XPASS-Vis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15629) | 14 Jun 2026 preprint; primary abstract/version history. | Cross-domain personalized aesthetics includes landscape images but still has a transfer gap. The abstract says data/code will be public upon acceptance; availability was not established. Do not assume it is a ready commercial training corpus. |
| S18 — [Discrete Preference Learning for Personalized Multimodal Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20434) | 22 Apr 2026; authors report SIGIR 2026 acceptance. Primary abstract. | Modality-specific graphs and discrete preference tokens are a possible later representation. Evidence is for multimodal generation; it does not justify a GNN as the first Cyrus model. |
| S19 — [CRED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08531) | 9 Mar 2026; ICRA 2026 stated in paper metadata. Primary abstract. | Generate comparisons that distinguish competing preference hypotheses. Robotics trajectories and environment design differ from aesthetic planting; test the acquisition idea under equal artist effort. |
| S20 — [Optimal Design for Active Preference Learning with Biased LLM Judges / NAOD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38860) | 30 Sep 2026, v1 preprint. Primary abstract. | Active acquisition may expose residual judge bias. Keep human anchors and audit selection. Very recent language-preference evidence; no visual/Cyrus reproduction or maturity claim. |
| S21 — [Direct Preference Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290) | 2023; primary abstract. | A later recipe language model could learn from context-matched preferred/rejected sequences. DPO does not directly train a C++ procedural evaluator or remove validity checks. |
| S22 — [Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760) | Original 2022; ICML 2023 proceedings also surfaced. Primary abstract. | Optimizing an imperfect reward too hard can worsen a more faithful target. Keep independent human evaluation and capped search. The paper's synthetic reward setup is not a measured Cyrus failure rate. |
| S23 — [Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization in Direct Alignment Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02900) | 2024; primary abstract. | Direct alignment can also overoptimize. Choosing DPO does not remove the need for evaluation, regularization and rollback. |
| S24 — [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) | 2023; primary abstract/overview. | Model judging needs bias checks and human comparison. Transfer to multimodal planting judgments must be measured rather than inferred from language evaluation. |

| S33 — [LangGraph interrupts](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/interrupts) | Current official documentation; replay and side-effect sections. | Resuming can rerun code before an interrupt. | S34 — [GraphRAG](https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/) | Current official documentation overview. | Text-derived entities, relationships and community summaries support retrieval. | S36 — [Writing effective tools for agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents) | 11 Sep 2025; official engineering guidance, overview/relevant tool-design material. | Give tools clear jobs and evaluate their usefulness. | S37 — [Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents) | 9 Jan 2026; official engineering article, definitions/grader sections. | Evaluate the real final state with deterministic, human and calibrated model checks. Keep scene interaction on the supported host path and copied-data inference outside. |
| S39 — [OpenTelemetry logging](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/otel/logs/) | Current official specification overview and correlation guidance. | Shared trace/span/resource context can correlate diagnostics. ## Most relevant reading sequence

Read S01 for domain fit; S09/S10/S11 for bounded search alternatives; S15/S17/S19/S20 for recent personalization and query-selection questions; S22/S23 for overoptimization; S38 for the host boundary; then S29–S33 and S37 for execution contracts and evaluation.

S03–S06 are retained because older procedural/vegetation work may be more directly useful than a new general agent paper. Publication recency is one criterion, not a ranking of engineering value.

## What remains unverified

No selected model has been benchmarked on the user's hardware, plant assets or references. No checkpoint is approved for shipping. Public availability of every cited dataset/codebase was not established, and paper licenses do not automatically cover data/assets/weights. No clinical, botanical or construction validity is inferred from visual synthesis results.

This review does not pick a permanently “best” foundation model. At implementation time, benchmark version-pinned local and hosted candidates on the same capability and artist-evaluation tasks. Recheck release notes, terms and costs then. Review new papers when they address a measured failure in the benchmark, rather than continually rebuilding around the newest architecture.
