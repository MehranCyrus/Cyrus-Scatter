# Cyrus Scatter 0.87.4.1 — interaction performance and reliability

**Status: scoped Max 2027 test build ready.** The requested patch improves shared source preparation, contact work and reporting while retaining the 0.87.4 preparation editor. It is an evidence-scoped test build, not a claim that every plant, collider or scene runs in real time. The user explicitly selected version 0.87.4.1; native Interactions advances to capability 9, saved scene schema remains 62, and independently versioned products do not change. Historical builds keep their original qualification.

## Installation and trial

The matched [Max 2027 MZP](../../../dist/CyrusScatter-0.87.4.1-20261027/CyrusScatter-0.87.4.1-Max2027.mzp) has SHA-256 `91616fe7c8806fe19cdf4d730846d56fcaad1d805df6ed062a2f5930571bf90a`. In Max 2027 choose **Scripting > Run Script**, select the MZP and restart Max. The artist profile was not installed into or restarted during the campaign.

The [small two-tree trial copy](../../../dist/CyrusScatter-0.87.4.1-20261027/Small_Tree_Interaction_0.87.4.1.max) retains the full tree meshes and uses separate lightweight trunk colliders. ## Changes and why

- Reuses prepared source geometry and guides only when source identity, saved definition, units, relevant settings and watched geometry still match. Different scattered instances keep their own transforms and poses. Source removal retains Manual-mode and Undo behavior.
- Uses a bounded spatial index to find colliders near a plant, including individual Cyrus Edit overrides, rather than scanning a large unrelated collider population. Work, temporary storage and worker reservations remain bounded.
- Distributes independent nearby-plant jobs across the existing bounded workers as each becomes available. Results and failure selection are still merged in stable input order.
- Reduces collision-query work for hinge checks only when a conservative predicate can decide clearance; ambiguous cases fall back to the original witness calculation. Actual visible-mesh validation remains independent and retains the full calculation.
- Detects collisions with fixed root edges and faces, even when every anchored vertex is outside. With root hiding disabled, an impossible fixed-root contact reports failure and retains the previous complete result; when enabled, confirmed covered rooted groups can be hidden.
- Compares a small bounded set of root-frame alternatives when a checked guide has high shape change. If a checked guide still exceeds the existing 25% edge-change diagnostic, Interactions points to **Check full visible mesh**. This warning covers only guides actually checked; its absence is not a full-mesh pass.
- Uses up to eight numeric participants automatically for sufficiently large interaction updates on the tested machine. Explicit session thread settings take precedence; the change does not alter ordinary scatter's automatic policy.

These are static fitted poses around colliders, not time-based dynamics or a wind simulation. Changing **Refinement stiffness** affects the iterative fallback solver but may have no visible effect when a faster hinge succeeds.

## Measurements and tests

The full 0.87.4 baseline attempted 33 real source models. The final matched package was exercised on 25 distinct real models with three moving-sphere contact directions apiece: **22 passed all three independent full-visible-mesh checks and move-away restoration, two need deformation review, and Tea Tree - 03 could not be prepared automatically**. The [per-model results](evidence-08741-candidate08/model-summary.csv) include geometry, settings and measured outcomes. An attempted test is not counted as a successful contact.

The exercised workflows also include 5%, 25% and 100% foliage previews, source removal and Manual-mode Undo/Redo, automatic Minimum stem and Branch connection updates, fixed-root hiding and contact gap, a box collider, separate tree-trunk proxies, the grass/fern preparation editor, Layers > Recipes opening, and two individual tree moves and Undo. The Library check used an empty private store; the renderer and every possible UI option or external model topology were not qualified.

An additional [source-switch stress check](evidence-08741-candidate08/source-switch-stress.json) used the final package in a disposable Max 2027 process with a non-handling access-violation recorder. Twelve cycles across four grass and broad-leaf sources completed **36 contacts and 36 independent full-mesh audits**, restored each moved-away plant, and rejected the sphere when deliberately selected as a reacting source. No access violation was captured in this run. The earlier intermittent fault remains undiagnosed; a clean repetition does not prove it cannot recur.

On a captured 12,416-reacting-placement terrace fixture with three Rock_10 collider instances, the original numerical kernel recorded 6.17 seconds for its initial fit and 5.65–6.42 seconds for three small movements. An intermediate improved kernel recorded about 2.07 seconds and 1.75–2.21 seconds for the corresponding positions and counts. The newer root-quality policy can select different validated poses, so this comparison does not claim identical output. In three alternating pairs of later builds, an additional clearance predicate changed median initial/first/second fit times from **1,642/1,090/1,250 ms** to **1,285/913/1,099 ms**, with identical recorded affected/hidden/fitted counts and numerical pose digests. A subsequent three-pair scheduling experiment changed those three medians from **1,358/995/1,154 ms** to **1,128/915/1,006 ms**, with matching pose digests, counts and warning counts. These are numerical replays on a Ryzen 5 5600X, not presented FPS, renderer time or a promise for other scenes.

In a separate synthetic locality fixture, 200 nearby plant bounds and 4,096 colliders, nearly all distant, took a median **17.10 ms** for the old fit stage and **0.34 ms** with the collider index. This isolates the cost of searching irrelevant colliders; it does not predict a Max scene with thousands of overlapping contacts. The index has its own accounted memory budget.

An actual Live-mode Cyrus Edit mouse drag of a scattered rock in the copied terrace showed a matching stone, gizmo and updated contact generation before mouse release. The final build still performs an additional procedural placement rebuild after release. During that concurrently loaded host run one observed held contact fit took about 1.13 seconds; it is not a clean comparative benchmark. An attempted shortcut was discarded because it would have left placement and MCP readback with the old stone position. A safe optimization requires an atomic updated placement publication as well as viewport/contact feedback.

During short real mouse-wheel zooms over the large terrace, the prior build recorded 30 displayed-frame samples with **44.70 ms** median / **142.81 ms** 95th-percentile displayed time; the final build recorded 28 samples with **48.31 / 129.46 ms**. Both began from the same checked viewport framing and used the same nine wheel events. These single, discrete sequences do not prove a viewport speedup or provide a continuous-navigation frame rate: displayed durations can include time between inputs when Max does not present a new frame. Placement and retained-buffer counters stayed unchanged in the final sequence. The scene used Mesh viewport mode, 5% interaction foliage preview, and idle/navigating triangle ceilings of 20 million/5 million; the retained display plan contained approximately 12.45 million/4.99 million triangles respectively. The plan is not a measured per-frame draw count. Frame timings come from the process-wide dominant swap chain, rather than isolated plugin GPU time or calibrated input-to-photon latency. See the [PresentMon metric definitions](https://github.com/GameTechDev/PresentMon/blob/main/README-ConsoleApplication.md), [viewport settings](evidence-08741-candidate08/viewport-settings.json), [baseline capture](evidence-0874-baseline/presentmon-wheel-baseline-summary.json) and [final capture](evidence-08741-candidate08/presentmon-wheel-final08-matched-summary.json).

Reducing **Idle triangles** to 2 million and **Navigating triangles** to 500,000 changed the retained plan to approximately 2.00 million/0.50 million triangles, without rebuilding placement. Some objects were represented by box proxies, visibly reducing fidelity; this is a draw budget, not a remesh of every model. One similarly framed nine-wheel sequence with that budget produced 25 displayed samples at **46.11 ms** median / **146.12 ms** 95th percentile. This small sample does not demonstrate a consistent speedup over the higher-budget sequence. The change is confined to the viewport preview; a final renderer image was not tested. [Budget and frame evidence](evidence-08741-candidate08/viewport-budget.json).

As a further control, temporarily disabling Scatter removed the visible plants and rock, yet one otherwise similar wheel sequence produced only 12 displayed samples with **142.77 ms** median displayed duration. This counterintuitive sparse sample cannot be subtracted from the enabled measurement or used to assign time to Scatter; it illustrates why an event-driven wheel test and per-frame duration alone are insufficient to diagnose responsiveness. A repeated continuous-input test with measured input-to-display latency remains necessary. [Control evidence](evidence-08741-candidate08/presentmon-wheel-no-scatter-summary.json).

Three short actual **Pan** drags changed the view while the placement and retained-buffer counters remained stable. Their input intervals contained only 11 displayed samples: **33.90 ms** median displayed duration and a **317.20 ms** maximum. These intervals include gesture transitions, and 11 frames are too few for a dependable percentile or a continuous-navigation guarantee. [Pan evidence](evidence-08741-candidate08/presentmon-pan-final08-summary.json).

The producer completed **60/60 native tests** and generated-source checks. The exact MZP passed archive, guard, payload-hash and disposable-profile installer-engine checks; automatic startup in Max 2027 loaded the matched script and modules. Detailed trial results, counterexamples and measurement limits are in [the investigation](INVESTIGATION.md), [engineering decisions](TECHNICAL_DECISIONS.md) and the linked evidence directories. Successful numerical and host tests do not establish a finished renderer image or presentation-frame rate.

## Known limits and next work

- **Common Blue Violet - 05** and **Lavender - 01** exceeded the 25% edge-change review threshold in particular tested contacts. One different **Arthropodium - 04** flower in the two-tree scene also needs review; alternate tested hinges did not improve that case without another tradeoff. This threshold is a diagnostic, not a guarantee of visual quality.
- **Tea Tree - 03** still has unresolved guide and foliage attachments even with raised preparation-work and guide limits. Manual source preparation or a different source is needed until its topology is supported. The exposed-root Tea Trees also need more than an automatic trunk-only proxy.
- One Max access violation occurred during a source-switch sequence. A clean repeat, five earlier interleaved cycles, and the twelve final-package cycles above did not reproduce it, but its cause was not identified. Treat it as an unresolved intermittent observation.
- Positive uniform interaction scales and closed collider meshes are required. Mirrored, sheared and nonuniform transforms, complex open collider surfaces, some fixed-root conflicts and extremely dense scenes require different handling or explicit proxies. The current limits include 16,384 global colliders, 64 relevant colliders per plant and 4,096 transient edited colliders; increasing resource limits does not repair topology.
- The largest measured contact remains roughly a second on this fixture, so fluid real-time performance is not established. The extra Live-mode placement rebuild and the stiffness control's limited effect are priorities for future design and measurement. Timeline animation, wind, automatic detailed tree-root colliders, full-renderer qualification and broad cross-version support are outside this delivery.

Original scenes, artist profiles and unrelated repository changes were preserved. No Git commit or push is included in this task.
